# Does *Khatam an-Nabiyyin* mean prophethood has ended? My notes

These are my notes on Quran 33:40. I don't think the title means every kind of prophethood has come to an end. Below are the reasons I find convincing, what people usually say back, and how I'd answer.

**Short version.** The four points I lead with are: (1) the Prophet ﷺ called the returning Jesus *Nabiyyullah*; (2) how the Prophet ﷺ used a seal; (3) Quran 7:35; (4) Musnad Ahmad 17163. Points 5–7 are backup. Point 5 needs the Arabic checked before I quote it (see "Still to check").

## Where we already agree

Everyone agrees that no new law can come, that the Quran is the last book, and that Muhammad ﷺ is the greatest of the prophets. The only question is whether the title also closes the door on a prophet who brings no new law, who follows the Prophet ﷺ and comes through him.

## The question I keep coming back to

Everybody reads "no prophet after me" with some exception. The mainstream view does too, because Jesus (as) is expected to return and is called a prophet. So the real question isn't whether there's an exception, it's which one.

Where does the Quran or the Prophet ﷺ say the exception is "only a prophet appointed before him", and not "only a prophet who brings a new law"?

---

## The four points I lead with

### 1. The Prophet ﷺ called the returning Jesus *Nabiyyullah*

In Muslim 2937a he describes what happens when the Messiah returns, and in the Arabic he says *Nabiyyullah*, the Prophet of Allah. (The English translation says "Allah's Messenger", so I'd read the Arabic.) So he used the word *nabi* for someone who appears after his death.

Usual reply: Jesus was appointed before Muhammad ﷺ and brings no new law, so this doesn't prove that a prophet can come after Muhammad ﷺ.

My answer: Quran 5:117 (*fa-lammā tawaffaytanī*) and 3:55 tell me Jesus died, so the Messiah who comes is from within the ummah. Bukhari 3449 says "your imam will be from among you".

### 2. How the Prophet ﷺ used a seal

In his time a seal (*khātam*) was what made a letter accepted. Bukhari 7162 says rulers wouldn't read a letter unless it was sealed, and al-Bukhari put it in a chapter on bearing witness to a sealed letter. Bukhari 5877 and Muslim 2092a say nobody else could copy his inscription. Bukhari 3106, 5873 and 5879 say Abu Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthman sealed with his ring after him. No new seal was made, everything went through his.

That's how I understand *zilli* prophethood: authority that only exists through his seal.

Conclusion: I guess we all agree on this?

Usual reply: the ring was used by caliphs (Bukhari 3455) and no hadith applies this to the title.

My answer: fair, it shows how the word was used, it doesn't define the title. I'd only use it that way.

### 3. Quran 7:35

> "O children of Adam, **if** messengers come to you from among yourselves…"

It's addressed to people in the Prophet's ﷺ time and in the future tense.

Usual reply: it's part of a narrative about past messengers, and "if" doesn't promise anything.

My answer: then show me the verse or hadith that takes the "if" away.

### 4. Musnad Ahmad 17163 / Ibn Hibban 6404

> "I was, with Allah, *Khatam an-Nabiyyin* while Adam was still in his clay."

Graded sahih by al-Albani and al-Hakim (al-Dhahabi agreed), and *sahih li-ghayrihi* by al-Arnaʾut. Nobody existed yet to come after, so I read the title as a rank and not a place in time.

Usual reply: "written in the Mother of the Book" means it was decreed that he'd be last.

My answer: that fits "seal" just as well.

## More reasons (backup)

### 5. Older scholars explained "no prophet after me" as "no law-bearing prophet"

- Mulla Ali al-Qari: *Khatam an-Nabiyyin* means no prophet after him who brings a new sharia (*al-Asrar al-Marfuʿa*, p. 192)
- Ibn Qutayba: no prophet who abrogates his law (*Taʾwil Mukhtalif al-Hadith*, p. 127)
- Ibn Arabi: prophethood hasn't been abolished completely, only the kind that brings a new law (*Futuhat*, vol. 3, p. 103)
- Shaʿrani, Barkhurdar and Shah Waliullah are quoted along the same lines

Usual reply: none of them accepted a new prophet, and al-Qari had Jesus's return in mind.

My answer: that still shows they didn't read the words as absolute. We're back to which exception.

*(Check the Arabic of these quotes before using them; see "Still to check".)*

### 6. Quran 4:69

> "Whoever obeys Allah and this Messenger will be *maʿa* those Allah has favoured: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous."

It's the same group we ask to follow in al-Fatiha (*alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim*), every day, in every prayer.

Usual reply: *maʿa* means "in company with", in the hereafter.

My answer: *maʿa* also means "among". In 4:146 "they are *maʿa* the believers" means among them, and so does "let us die *maʿa* the righteous" in 3:193. If it only meant company, the same would apply to the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous, and nobody says those can't be reached in this life.

### 7. Quran 40:34

Joseph's people said "Allah will never send a messenger after him", and the Quran says Allah leaves astray whoever is a transgressor and a doubter. To me it shows the Quran treating "the door is shut" as a human assumption.

Usual reply: it's about Joseph, and the Seal is given separately.

## What's usually quoted on the other side

- **The brick hadith** (Bukhari 3535): the house is the law, and we all agree he completed it. It doesn't say no one can come after who follows it.
- **Tabuk** (Bukhari 4416): the exception is about Ali, a deputy like Aaron but not a prophet. It isn't about the whole ummah.
- **Thirty liars** (Abu Dawud 4252): a warning about fakes assumes there's a real one. And he called the returning Jesus *nabi*.
- **Bukhari 3455** ("no prophet after me, but there will be caliphs"): it's about who governs the community (*tasūsuhum*).
- **Bukhari 6990** ("nothing remains of prophethood except *mubashshirāt*"): it says a part of prophethood remains.
- **Bukhari 6194** (Ibn Abi Awfa: if there were a prophet after Muhammad, Ibrahim would have lived): that's a Companion's opinion, not the Prophet ﷺ. Ibn Abbas reports otherwise (Ibn Majah 1511), though see my note on that below.
- **Arabic dictionaries** (*Lisan al-Arab*, *Taj al-ʿArus*) say *khatam* can mean "last": I agree it can. I'd ask "last in what sense?" and go to point 5.
- **Consensus:** al-Qari, Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Arabi and others read it more narrowly, so I'm not sure the agreement on the absolute reading is as complete as people say.

## Things I'm careful with

- I don't say *khatam* never means "last". The dictionaries say it can.
- I don't say the root never means "end" in the Quran. Al-Tabari reads 83:26 as "end", and the clear uses of the root (2:7, 36:65) are about shutting things.
- ʿAʾisha's saying ("say *Khatam an-Nabiyyin*, don't say there's no prophet after him") isn't a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ and its chain is disputed. If I mention it, I call it her statement.
- Ibn Majah 1511 (Ibrahim "would have been a prophet") is weak according to some and al-Nawawi rejected it. People will answer with Bukhari 6194.

## Two questions to keep coming back to

1. Which exception to "no prophet after me" is the right one, and why is "no law-bearing prophet" less valid than "no one who wasn't already a prophet"?
2. Where is the text that closes it absolutely? Not a gloss on *khatam*, but a text saying no prophet of any kind, ever, after him.
