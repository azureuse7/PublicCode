# 3. The Death of Jesus (Wafāt-e-Masīḥ) and His "Second Coming"

*Part 3 of 6 in a series on the main points of dispute between Ahmadi Muslims and mainstream Muslims. It covers the meaning of* tawaffā *and* rafʿ*, the crucifixion verses, the descent hadith and the case on both sides. It describes the debate; it does not decide it.*

> **Note on verse numbers.** Standard numbering is used. In an Ahmadi Quran most verses are one number higher.

---

## At a glance

| | Most mainstream Muslims | Ahmadis (both branches) | Most Christians |
|---|---|---|---|
| The crucifixion | Jesus was not crucified; most classical commentators say someone else was made to look like him and was crucified instead | Jesus was put on the cross but did not die on it; he was taken down unconscious and recovered | He died on the cross and rose bodily on the third day |
| Afterwards | God raised him, body and soul, alive to heaven | He left Palestine, travelled east to find the lost tribes of Israel, and died a natural death in old age (Ahmadis place his grave in Srinagar, Kashmir) | He ascended to heaven |
| The "second coming" | The same Jesus will come down before the end of time | Fulfilled spiritually by a man of the Muslim ummah: Mirza Ghulam Ahmad | The same Jesus will return |

---

## 1. Why this matters so much

This is the doctrinal engine of the whole disagreement. The logic runs:

- If Jesus is physically alive in heaven, the promised Messiah must be Jesus himself, and anyone else claiming the office is mistaken.
- If Jesus died a natural death, the prophecy of his return must be fulfilled by someone *resembling* him, and the question becomes who.

For Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, Jesus's natural death was a foundation stone:

- **Against Christian doctrine.** If Jesus did not die on the cross, the doctrines of atonement and resurrection lose their base. This is how Ahmadis understand the hadith that the Messiah will "break the cross".
- **For the honour of Muhammad.** If Jesus alone among the prophets has lived in heaven for two thousand years without ageing, while Muhammad lies buried in Medina, Jesus seems to have a unique, superhuman status. Christian missionaries in 19th-century India used exactly this idea in their preaching.
- **For his own claim.** If Jesus has died and will not come back in person, the promised "son of Mary" must be someone else.

Ahmadi literature often sums this up in a saying attributed to him: let Jesus die, for in his death lies the life of Islam.

For the same reason, Ahmadis regard the death of Jesus as their most important argument, and many prefer to begin a discussion here rather than with 33:40 (the "seal of the prophets" verse, Part 1), because the case can be made from plain Quranic language.

## 2. The Quranic verses, one by one

### 3:55

> إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَىٰ إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ

"When Allah said: O Jesus, I will *take* you (*mutawaffīka*) and raise you to Myself…"

The question turns on *mutawaffīka*, from the root *w-f-y* (form V).

- **Ahmadi:** *mutawaffīka* means "I will cause you to die". Al-Bukhārī, in his Book of Tafsir, records Ibn ʿAbbās explaining *mutawaffīka* as *mumītuka*, "I will cause you to die". Mirza Ghulam Ahmad argued that whenever God is the subject and a human being the object, *tawaffā* means taking the soul: in death, or in sleep when sleep is mentioned. He offered a public reward to anyone who could show otherwise from Arabic usage.
  - Ahmadis count roughly two dozen such occurrences, among them 2:234, 2:240, 4:15, 4:97, 6:61, 7:37, 7:126, 8:50, 10:46, 10:104, 12:101, 13:40, 16:28, 16:32, 16:70, 22:5, 32:11, 40:67, 40:77 and 47:27, and argue that none of them means removal while alive.
  - They read 39:42 ("Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and of those that die not, during their sleep") as confirming the point: sleep is named explicitly as a separate case because it is not the default sense. Where the Quran means sleep, it says so.
  - The order also matters: death is promised first, then "raising" (*rafʿ*), which means honour and nearness to God. Compare Idrīs ("We raised him to a high station", 19:57), and the prayer Muslims say between the two prostrations, *warfaʿnī*, "and raise me".
- **Mainstream:** *tawaffā* comes from *wafā'*, "completeness", and means "to take in full"; what is taken may be the whole person, body and soul together. The Quran uses it for sleep (6:60; 39:42), so it is not confined to death. God took Jesus fully and raised him. Some early authorities said the "and" does not show order, so the raising came first and death will come after his return; others said *tawaffā* here means sleep. Other reports from Ibn ʿAbbās say Jesus was raised alive.
- **Ahmadi rejoinder:** if "taking in full" without death were an available sense, the Quran would show at least one instance of it applied to a living person. Ahmadis say it does not.

### 4:157–158

> وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ وَلَٰكِن شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ … بَل رَّفَعَهُ اللَّهُ إِلَيْهِ

"They did not kill him and did not crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them… and they did not kill him for certain; rather, Allah raised him to Himself."

- **Mainstream:** Jesus was never put on the cross to die. Most classical commentators report that someone else (a volunteer disciple, or Judas, in different accounts) was made to look like him and was crucified in his place, while Jesus was lifted bodily to heaven, where he remains alive. The particle *bal* ("rather, on the contrary") signals a real alternative to being killed, and the alternative given is the *rafʿ*.
- **Ahmadi:** *ṣalaba* means to *kill* by crucifixion, not simply to fasten to a cross. So "they did not *ṣalabūhu*" means they did not succeed in killing him on the cross, and *shubbiha lahum* means he was made to *appear* dead to them. The background is the Jewish claim to have killed him on the cross, which under Deuteronomy 21:23 ("anyone hung on a tree is under God's curse") would prove him accursed. The Quran replies that he did not die that accursed death; instead God "raised" him, that is, honoured him. Ahmadis add that the substitution story makes God the author of a deception and punishes an innocent man.
  - For the honorific sense of *rafʿ* they cite 19:57 (Idrīs: "We exalted him to a lofty station"), 35:10 ("to Him ascend good words") and 94:4 ("and We exalted your name", of the Prophet).
  - They also argue that God is not located in a place, so "raised *to Allah*" cannot describe movement through space without implying that God occupies a direction, which both communities reject.
- **Mainstream reply:** the honorific sense exists, but here the contrast is explicitly with being killed, so the verse answers *what happened to him physically*, not *what his rank was*.

### 4:159

"There is none of the People of the Book but will believe in him (or it) before his death…"

- **Mainstream (a major view):** before *Jesus's* death. After his return, all the People of the Book will believe in him.
- **Ahmadi:** before *their own* death, each will believe "in it" (in the crucifixion as they understand it), or will recognise the truth about Jesus at the moment of death. Al-Ṭabarī records early authorities who said the pronoun refers to the death of each person of the Book, and a non-canonical reading attributed to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb has "before *their* death".

### 5:116–117

"…I was a witness over them as long as I remained among them; but when You *took me* (*falammā tawaffaytanī*), You Yourself were the Watcher over them."

- **Ahmadi:** Ahmadis consider this the single strongest verse. On the Day of Judgment Jesus will say that his followers went astray (by making him divine) *after* God "took" him, and that his knowledge of them ended at that point. Christians made him divine long ago; if he were still alive, or had returned to live among Muslims, there would have been no such break and his answer would not be true, so he must have died. The Prophet used the very same words for his own death: al-Bukhārī (3349, 4625) records that on that Day, when some of his followers are led away, he will say "as the righteous servant said: … *falammā tawaffaytanī*".
- **Mainstream:** *tawaffaytanī* refers to his being raised up, or to his death after his return, which in any case comes before Judgment Day. The verse does not decide when he died.

### 3:144

"Muhammad is only a messenger; messengers (*al-rusul*) have passed away (*khalat*) before him…"

- **Ahmadi:** when the Prophet died, ʿUmar and others could not accept it, and Abū Bakr settled the matter by reciting this verse (al-Bukhārī 4454 and related reports). Ahmadis call this the Companions' first consensus: *all* earlier messengers had died, so Muhammad could die too. That argument would fail if Jesus were still alive.
- **Mainstream:** *khalat* means "passed on" or "went by" (messengers do not stay with their people for ever) and does not have to mean that every one of them has died.

### 5:75

"The Messiah son of Mary was only a messenger; messengers had passed away before him; his mother was truthful (*ṣiddīqah*); they both *used to* eat food."

- **Ahmadi:** *khalat* is the Quran's standard idiom for having died and gone (as in 3:144). "Used to eat" (*kānā yaʾkulān*) implies that they no longer do, and eating is mentioned as proof of ordinary mortal humanity, which would be pointless if Jesus were exempt from death. Read with 21:8 ("We did not give them bodies that ate no food, nor were they immortal"), it shows that Jesus no longer lives a bodily life.
- **Mainstream:** the past tense describes their life on earth in order to prove they were human. It says nothing about Jesus's present state.

*The same verse calls Mary* ṣiddīqah *("truthful"), which settles the separate question of her status.*

### Other verses Ahmadis cite

- **21:34.** "We have not given everlasting life to any human being before you."
- **23:15.** "Then, after that, you will surely die."
- **19:31.** "He has commanded me to pray and give charity as long as I live." How could one give charity in heaven?
- **23:50.** "We gave them (Jesus and his mother) refuge on high ground (*rabwa*) with meadows and springs." Ahmadis identify this with Kashmir; classical commentators suggested Damascus, Ramla or Egypt, linking it to Jesus's birth or childhood.
- **43:61.** "He (or it) is a sign of the Hour." *Mainstream:* Jesus's descent is a sign of the Hour. *Ahmadi:* "it" is the Quran, or Jesus's birth without a father, as a sign that prophethood was passing from the Israelites. Ahmadis also note the variant reading *ʿalam* ("sign") for *ʿilm*, and in either case read the verse as the Messianic advent marking an era, not as proof of physical survival.
- **3:46.** "He will speak to people in the cradle and in middle age (*kahl*)." Some mainstream commentators argue that Jesus was raised before middle age, so this awaits his return; Ahmadis reply that he reached old age on earth.

## 3. The hadith about the Messiah's descent

### The mainstream reading

Many hadith speak of the descent (*nuzūl*) of "the son of Mary":

- "By Him in whose hand is my soul, the son of Mary will soon descend among you as a just ruler; he will break the cross, kill the swine and abolish the jizya" (al-Bukhārī 3448; Muslim 155).
- He will descend by the white minaret east of Damascus, wearing two lightly dyed garments, his hands on the wings of two angels, and kill the Dajjal at the gate of Ludd (Muslim 2937).
- He will pray behind the Muslims' leader (Muslim 156) and perform the pilgrimage (Muslim 1252).
- He will remain forty years, then die and receive a Muslim funeral (Abū Dāwūd 4324).

Scholars such as Ibn Kathīr and Anwar Shah Kashmiri (*al-Taṣrīḥ bimā tawātara fī nuzūl al-Masīḥ*) considered these reports *mutawātir* in meaning: transmitted by so many chains that the core cannot be doubted even where details vary. This underpins the mainstream consensus, supported by 43:61 and 4:159 as read above.

### The Ahmadi reading of the same hadith

- **"Descent" need not mean from the sky.** The Quran uses the same verb (*anzala*, "sent down") for iron (57:25), clothing (7:26), cattle (39:6) and the Prophet himself (65:10–11), meaning something sent with God's favour. None of these fell from the sky. A guest who arrives is a *nazīl*.
- **Two different descriptions.** In al-Bukhārī, the Jesus the Prophet met during the Ascension (*Miʿrāj*) is reddish with curly hair (3437–3438), while the Messiah he saw in a vision circling the Kaʿba is brown-skinned with straight hair (3440). The Companion Ibn ʿUmar even swore that the Prophet did not call Jesus "red" in that vision (3441). Ahmadis conclude that these are two people: the Israelite Jesus and the coming Messiah.
- **"Your imam from among you."** "How will you be when the son of Mary comes down among you and your imam is from among you?" (al-Bukhārī 3449); a version in Muslim (155) reads "and he will lead you, from among you". Ahmadis take this to mean that the Messiah himself will be "from among you", a Muslim of this ummah. On the mainstream reading the phrase has to refer to a *second* person (the Mahdi) whom Jesus follows; Ahmadis argue the more natural reading is that the Messiah is the one from among you.
- **Messiah and Mahdi are one:** "There is no Mahdi except Jesus" (Ibn Mājah 4039).
- **Symbolic tasks:** "breaking the cross" means refuting the doctrine of the crucifixion and atonement by argument (Ahmadis connect this to Mirza Ghulam Ahmad's writings against Christian missionary claims); "killing the swine" means overcoming moral corruption by argument; "abolishing the jizya" (or, in a variant reading Ahmadis cite, "ending war", *yaḍaʿu l-ḥarb*) means there will be no religious wars in his time.
- **The Ascension vision.** In the Miʿrāj narrations (al-Bukhārī 3887) the Prophet sees Jesus in the second heaven with John the Baptist (Yaḥyā), and Adam, Joseph, Idrīs, Aaron, Moses and Abraham in other heavens. Every other prophet in that vision had died. If the vision shows souls in the hereafter, it shows Jesus among the dead; if it shows living bodies, it would prove that Adam and Moses are physically alive too, which nobody claims.
- **Jesus's lifespan:** a report in al-Ṭabarānī (collected in *Kanz al-ʿUmmāl*) says Jesus lived 120 years.

### Mainstream replies

- The two descriptions are of the same Jesus seen on different occasions.
- "Your imam from among you" refers to the Muslim leader (often identified with the Mahdi) who leads the prayer while Jesus follows.
- The descent is described with physical details (the minaret, the angels' wings) that resist a symbolic reading.
- The 120-year report is weak.
- The Ascension vision shows each prophet in the station appropriate to him; it does not establish a uniform mode of presence or say anything about the state of their bodies.

## 4. Arguments from the Gospels

In *Masih Hindustan Mein* (*Jesus in India*; written 1899, published 1908) Mirza Ghulam Ahmad argued from the New Testament itself that Jesus survived:

- Jesus prayed earnestly to be spared the "cup" (Matthew 26:39), and Hebrews 5:7 says his prayer was heard.
- He compared his coming ordeal to the sign of Jonah (Matthew 12:40), and Jonah was *alive* inside the fish.
- He was on the cross only a few hours; Pilate was surprised that he was already dead (Mark 15:44).
- His legs were not broken, unlike those of the two men crucified with him (John 19:32–33).
- Blood and water flowed when his side was pierced (John 19:34), which Ahmadis take as a sign of life.
- Pilate's wife urged Pilate not to harm him after a dream (Matthew 27:19), and the body was given to sympathetic followers, Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus, who brought about a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes (John 19:38–39).
- Afterwards he showed his wounds, said he had flesh and bones, and ate fish (Luke 24:39–43): a living human body. He also avoided his enemies and met his disciples in secret.
- He had said he was sent "only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matthew 15:24) and had "other sheep that are not of this fold" (John 10:16), which Ahmadis link to the scattered Israelite tribes in the East.
- Ahmadis also point to the *marham-e-ʿĪsā* ("ointment of Jesus") mentioned in old medical books as a remedy for wounds.

*Christian scholars' reply:* Roman crucifixion was designed to kill and the soldiers confirmed death; the Gospel writers present the appearances as those of a risen, not a recovering, Jesus; and the "swoon" theory has been widely rejected in New Testament scholarship.

## 5. The Elijah precedent

The Book of Malachi (4:5) promised that the prophet Elijah would come back before "the day of the Lord". When asked about this, Jesus said that John the Baptist *was* the Elijah who was to come (Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13), and the angel said John would go "in the spirit and power of Elijah" (Luke 1:17). Jewish scholars who expected the literal Elijah rejected this. Mirza Ghulam Ahmad argued that Muslims waiting for the literal return of Jesus were repeating their mistake: a promised "return" is fulfilled by another person in the spirit of the first.

*Mainstream reply:* the Islamic texts name the person (Jesus son of Mary), the place and the deeds; the Elijah comparison comes from scriptures Muslims regard as altered; and nothing in the hadith suggests a substitute.

## 6. The Kashmir thesis

Mirza Ghulam Ahmad argued that after recovering, Jesus travelled east (through Nisibis, Afghanistan and the Punjab) to Kashmir, where he taught under the name Yuz Asaf and died at around 120 years of age. He identified the **Roza Bal** shrine in the Khanyar area of Srinagar as his tomb, citing local tradition and older Persian and Kashmiri texts about a foreign prophet called Yuz Asaf. One of his associates collected the signatures of some 556 local residents attesting the tradition.

This is best presented as a **community claim, not an established finding**: mainstream Muslim scholars reject it, and academic historians generally regard the identification as unsupported.

**Objections:**

- The shrine's custodians and local Muslims regard it as the tomb of a Muslim holy man, Yuz Asaf; it also holds the grave of Mir Sayyid Naseeruddin.
- Several scholars, for example Günter Grönbold (1985) and Norbert Klatt (1988), argue that the name Yuz Asaf comes from *Yūdhāsaf/Būdhāsaf*, the hero of the *Barlaam and Josaphat* legend, a Christian and Muslim retelling of the life of the Buddha. On this view, the old texts describe the Buddha, not Jesus.

**Ahmadi reply:** Ahmadi researchers have produced further manuscripts and traditions in support, and many stress that the Quranic case for Jesus's natural death does not depend on identifying his grave.

## 7. Muslim scholars outside Ahmadiyya who held that Jesus died

- **Ibn ʿAbbās**, in the tafsir material in al-Bukhārī, glosses *mutawaffīka* as *mumītuka*, "causing you to die" (see 3:55 above).
- **Imam Mālik** is reported by Ibn Ḥazm to have held that Jesus died.
- **Wahb ibn Munabbih** held that Jesus died for a period before being raised.
- **Maḥmūd Shaltūt**, later Grand Imam of al-Azhar (1958–63), published a fatwa in the Cairo journal *al-Risāla* (1942) saying that nothing in the Quran or the sound sunna requires believing that Jesus was taken up bodily and is alive in heaven. He read *tawaffā* as natural death and *rafʿ* as honour, and held that someone who denies the bodily ascension is not a disbeliever.
- Ahmadi compilations also cite Rashīd Riḍā's *Tafsīr al-Manār* (which, they report, treated a flight to India and death there as not unreasonable), Muṣṭafā al-Marāghī and others.

None of these scholars accepted Mirza Ghulam Ahmad's claims; Shaltūt, for example, made only the exegetical point and did not endorse any conclusion about who fulfils the prophecy. Ahmadis cite them to show that "Jesus died" can be held within Sunni scholarship. *Mainstream reply:* this has remained a minority view, and major scholarly bodies continue to affirm the traditional belief.

## 8. The disagreement stated precisely

> **Does *tawaffā* in 3:55 and 5:117 mean death, and is the *nuzūl* (descent) of the hadith physical or metaphorical?**

Every other element of this topic follows from those two questions.

## 9. Summary

| Point | Mainstream | Ahmadi |
|---|---|---|
| Was Jesus crucified? | Not placed on the cross; another was made to look like him | Placed on it, but did not die on it |
| Is he alive now? | Yes, bodily, in heaven | No; he died a natural death |
| 3:55, *mutawaffīka* | "I will take you in full", body and soul | "I will cause you to die" (Ibn ʿAbbās, in al-Bukhārī) |
| *Rafʿ* (raising) | Bodily elevation | Elevation of rank and honour |
| 4:157 | Not crucified at all | Not killed *on* the cross; he only appeared dead |
| 5:117 | After his raising, or his death after his return | Christians went astray after his death |
| 3:144 | "Passed on", not necessarily died | All earlier messengers had died (Abū Bakr's speech) |
| The descent hadith | *Mutawātir*; physical, detailed, literal, still to come | Symbolic; fulfilled by a man of this ummah |
| "Your imam from among you" | The Mahdi, whom Jesus follows | The Messiah himself |
| The Gospels | He died and rose (Christian view) | He survived and was treated |
| Kashmir | A local saint's tomb; a Buddha legend | Roza Bal is his tomb |

## Practical note

This is generally seen as the strongest ground for the Ahmadi position, because the argument can be made almost entirely from the Quran in plain language, and because it does not require the listener to accept anything about Mirza Ghulam Ahmad first.

Two cautions apply to anyone presenting it. Do not lean on the Srinagar tomb: it is contested and not necessary. And acknowledge openly that the descent narrations are numerous and were understood literally by the early community; the Ahmadi case has to meet that fact with the argument about *nuzūl* and *imāmukum minkum*, not by pretending the hadith are thin.

## Key terms

- **Wafāt-e-Masīḥ**: the death of the Messiah.
- **Tawaffā**: to take in full; to cause to die.
- **Rafʿ**: raising, exaltation.
- **Nuzūl**: descent, coming down.
- **Mutawātir**: a report transmitted by so many people that error is ruled out.
- **Shubbiha lahum**: "it was made to appear so to them".
