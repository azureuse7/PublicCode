## Page 262 — English translation

(1) The first is the era of the **completion of guidance** (*takmīl-e-hidāyat*), which this verse points to: "reciting purified pages, wherein are lasting writings" [98:2–3]. (2) The second is the era of the **completion of propagation** (*takmīl-e-ishā'at*), which the verse "that He may make it prevail over every religion" [61:9] points to.

Just as it was the Holy Prophet's (peace be upon him) duty, because prophethood was sealed in him, to complete the guidance, so too, because his law was universal, it was his duty to complete its propagation throughout the whole world.

But although the guidance was completed in the Holy Prophet's time, as the verse "This day I have perfected your religion for you" [5:3] and the verse "reciting purified pages, wherein are lasting writings" [98:2–3] both bear witness, at that time the **completion of the propagation of guidance was impossible**.

There was no good arrangement for carrying the faith to people of other languages, for explaining its proofs to them, or even for meeting them. The countries of the world were so cut off from each other that every nation more or less believed there was no other land besides its own. The Hindus, for instance, thought there were no people living beyond the Himalayas. Travel was not easy either, and ships could only sail when the wind was right.

**So God Almighty postponed the completion of propagation to an age** in which nations came into contact with one another; in which land and sea transport appeared that could hardly be bettered for ease of travel; and in which the abundance of printing presses made books like sweets that could be handed out to every gathering in the world.

So at this time, as the verse "And others from among them who have not yet joined them" [62:3] states, and as the verse "Say: O mankind, I am the Messenger of Allah to you all" [7:158] states, **there arose the need for a second advent (*dūsrā ba'th*) of the Holy Prophet** (peace be upon him).

And all these servants — the railway, the telegraph, the steamship, the printing presses, the excellent postal system, the knowledge of one another's languages, and especially, in India, Urdu, which had become a common language between Hindus and Muslims — petitioned the Holy Prophet (peace be upon him), in the language of circumstance, …

## Page 263 — English translation (main text)

… "O Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him), all of us servants are at your service, and we are eager, heart and soul, to fulfil the **duty of propagation**. Please come and fulfil this duty of yours, for your claim is: 'I have come for all mankind.' Now is the time when you can convey the Qur'an's message to every nation living on earth, bring its propagation to perfection, and spread the proofs of the Qur'an's truth among all people so the argument is complete."

## Page 263 — English translation (footnote ☆) — the key passage

☆ Since the Holy Prophet's (peace be upon him) **second official duty (*dūsrā farz-e-manṣabī*), namely the completion of the propagation of guidance**, was impossible in his time because the means of propagation did not exist, the Holy Qur'an in the verse "And others from among them who have not yet joined them" [62:3] gave the promise of the Holy Prophet's **second coming (*āmad-e-thānī*)**.

The need for this promise arose precisely because **the Holy Prophet's second official duty — completing the propagation of the guidance of the faith — which ought to have been fulfilled at his own hand, was not fulfilled at that time owing to the lack of means** (*jo āp ke hāth se pūrā honā chāhiye thā us waqt ba-bā'is-e-'adam-e-wasā'il pūrā nahīn huā*).

So the Holy Prophet fulfilled this duty through his second coming, which was in the colour of *burūz*, in an age when the means had come into being to carry Islam to all the nations of the earth. — *[note by the author]*

## Explanation

### 1. Does the page say the Prophet "failed"?

**The word "failed" (*nākām*) does not appear on either page.** The exact phrase, in the footnote on p. 263, is *pūrā nahīn huā*: "**was not fulfilled / not completed**". The page attributes this to "the lack of means" (*'adam-e-wasā'il*), not to any shortcoming in the Prophet.

So the accurate statement is that he wrote that the Prophet's **second duty — worldwide propagation — "ought to have been fulfilled at his hand" but "was not fulfilled at that time" because the means did not exist.** Whether that amounts to saying the Prophet "failed in his task" is the point of dispute.

### 2. The argument step by step

1. Islam's era of ascendancy has two phases: **completion of guidance** and **completion of propagation**.
2. The Prophet had **two duties**: as the Seal of Prophets, to complete the guidance; and because his law is universal, to complete its spread throughout the world.
3. **Duty 1 was completed** in his lifetime (Qur'an 5:3, "This day I have perfected your religion").
4. **Duty 2 was impossible then**: no means to reach other languages and lands, nations isolated, travel slow, ships dependent on wind.
5. So **God postponed** completion of propagation to the age of railways, telegraph, steamships, printing presses and postal systems.
6. In that age, per Qur'an 62:3 and 7:158, a **"second advent" of the Prophet** was needed.
7. The modern inventions, personified, "petition" the Prophet to come and finish his duty. The **Prophet's spirit replies that he will come by *burūz*, in India**.
8. Since a literal return was impossible, the Prophet's spirit **chose a person resembling him and figuratively gave him the names Ahmad and Muhammad**. In context, this refers to Mirza Ghulam Ahmad himself.
9. The footnote states the conclusion directly: the second duty was not fulfilled in the Prophet's time and is fulfilled through his *burūzī* second coming.

### 3. How critics read it

Mainstream Sunni and Shia scholars, and anti-Ahmadi writers, raise these objections:

- **It implies the Prophet's mission was left incomplete**, with a duty that "ought to have been fulfilled at his hand" left undone. They see this as contradicting Qur'an 5:3 and the Farewell Sermon, where the Prophet asked "Have I conveyed?" and the people affirmed he had.
- **It makes Mirza the completer of the Prophet's work.** Combined with being given the names "Muhammad and Ahmad" and described as the Prophet's "appearance", they argue this is a claim to a share in the Prophet's own station, and so a breach of *Khatm-e-Nubuwwat* (finality of prophethood).
- The stated reason, lack of technology, does not remove the objection for them. Their view is that the Prophet's duty was *tablīgh* (to convey the message), which he fully discharged. The later spread of Islam is the work of the *ummah*, not an unfinished duty of the Prophet.

### 4. How Ahmadis read it

- The passage itself says **guidance was fully completed** in the Prophet's time. Only the *physical, worldwide spread* awaited later means. That Islam had not reached the Americas or most of Europe by 632 CE is a historical fact, not a criticism.
- The delay is attributed to **God's decision** ("God Almighty postponed it"), not to any failing of the Prophet.
- The "second coming" is **by *burūz*** — a reflection of the Prophet through a follower, done "figuratively" (*majāzī*) — so in their view it is the Prophet's own work continuing, not a rival prophet completing it.
- Mainstream Muslims also expect Islam's worldwide dominance in the end times through the Mahdi or the Messiah. Ahmadis say Mirza is describing that same expectation.

### 5. Bottom line

- **What the text clearly says:** the Prophet's second duty, completing the worldwide spread of guidance, "ought to have been fulfilled at his hand" but "was not fulfilled at that time owing to the lack of means". It was fulfilled through his *burūzī* second coming. In context, that second coming refers to Mirza Ghulam Ahmad himself.
- **What it does not say:** that the Prophet "failed". That word is not there, and the same passage says the guidance and religion were completed in his lifetime.
- **The disagreement** is whether "a duty that should have been fulfilled at his hand was not fulfilled" means the same thing as "he failed in his task". Critics say it does in substance. Ahmadis say it does not.
