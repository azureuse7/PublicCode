# Takfir and Hostility Between Salafi, Deobandi, Barelvi and Wahhabi Movements

Nov 5, 2026 · @Gautam

There is well-documented evidence that Salafi/Wahhabi, Deobandi and Barelvi scholars have declared each other unbelievers or deviants, and that the rivalry has turned violent in Pakistan. The clearest cases are formal declarations of unbelief (*takfir*) between Barelvis and Deobandis, and the early Wahhabi movement treating other Muslims as polytheists. Many other rulings stop short of takfir but still brand the other side as deviant.

## Formal declarations of unbelief (takfir)

- **Barelvi against Deobandi and Ahl-e-Hadith (1906).**
  - Ahmad Raza Khan's [*Husam al-Haramayn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husamul_Haramain) declared three Deobandi founders unbelievers: Qasim Nanautawi, Rashid Ahmad Gangohi and Ashraf Ali Thanwi. He objected to writings he saw as blasphemous towards God, the Prophet and the saints.
  - It also condemned the Ahl-e-Hadith movement and the founder of the Ahmadiyya.
  - He gathered endorsements from 33 scholars, building on a draft he prepared while visiting Mecca and Medina in 1905.
- **The Deobandi reply (1907).** Khalil Ahmad Saharanpuri's [*al-Muhannad 'ala al-Mufannad*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Muhannad_ala_al-Mufannad) rebutted the charges point by point, answering questions from scholars in Mecca and Medina. It covered Wahhabis, Mawlid, intercession and more, and senior Deobandi scholars signed it.
- **Early Wahhabis against other Muslims (18th–19th century).**
  - Historian Cole Bunzel argues the early Wahhabis saw Muslims who venerated saints' graves as polytheists. They treated declaring them unbelievers as a duty, and eventually fought non-Wahhabi Muslims as jihad.
  - He adds that most Muslims regarded Wahhabism as a radical heresy for over 150 years ([Hoover Institution interview](https://www.hoover.org/node/91430); his book *Wahhābism: The History of a Militant Islamic Movement*, Princeton UP, 2023).
- **A Barelvi fatwa in practice (India, 2013).**
  - In a village in Sultanpur, Uttar Pradesh, a Deobandi cleric led a funeral prayer.
  - A fatwa from a Bareilly seminary then declared void the marriages of the village men who had prayed behind him, about 150 couples, and told them to remarry.
  - Other clerics condemned it, including the president of the UP Sunni Waqf Board ([DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/india/report-150-muslim-couples-caught-in-clerics-crossfire-1120316)).

## Rulings that stop short of takfir but brand the other side deviant

- **Salafi against Barelvi.** The Salafi fatwa site [IslamQA](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/150265) calls the Barelvis an "extreme Sufi sect." It says many of their practices are marks of disbelief and religious innovation. It also cites a Saudi Standing Committee ruling that praying behind such an imam is not permissible.
- **Deobandi against Barelvi.**
  - Two seminaries issued fatwas responding to a call for Deobandi–Barelvi unity: Darul Uloom Deoband (around 1997) and Mazahir Uloom Saharanpur ([Deoband.org](https://www.deoband.org/2010/07/theology-rulings/a-call-to-deobandi-barelwi-unity-fatwas-from-two-leading-deobandi-institutes/)).
  - They call Barelvi additions to belief and practice impermissible innovations, and forbid attending Barelvi Mawlid and *urs* (saints' anniversary) gatherings.
  - The Saharanpur fatwa says counting Barelvis as part of mainstream Sunni Islam (Ahl al-Sunnah) is wrong, though Deoband adds that its elders avoided takfir.
  - Another Darul Uloom Deoband fatwa allows a Deobandi woman to marry a Barelvi man only if there's hope he will repent of Barelvi beliefs ([IslamQA.org, fatwa 1047/1431](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/darulifta-deoband/24316/is-it-possible-for-a-deobandi-girl-to-marry-a-barelvi-boy/)).
- **The Saudi religious establishment against Tablighi Jamaat (2021).**
  - Tablighi Jamaat grew out of Deobandi circles.
  - In December 2021, Saudi Arabia's Minister of Islamic Affairs had Friday sermons warn against it as "one of the gates of terrorism" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/danger-to-society-saudi-arabia-bans-tablighi-jamaat/780500/)).
  - Darul Uloom Deoband called the accusations of polytheism (*shirk*), innovation and terrorism baseless, and demanded they be withdrawn ([Ummid](https://ummid.com/news/2021/december/12.12.2021/darul-uloom-deoband-condemns-saudi-arabia-for-linking-tablighi-jamaat-with-terrorism.html)).
- **Sufi-leaning Sunnis against Salafis (2016).**
  - A conference in Grozny, opened by Al-Azhar's Grand Imam, defined Sunni Islam as the Ash'ari and Maturidi creeds, the four legal schools and Sufism. This effectively left Salafis out.
  - Saudi Arabia's Council of Senior Scholars condemned it ([AGSI](https://agsi.org/analysis/who-is-sunni-chechnya-islamic-conference-opens-window-on-intra-faith-rivalry/)).

## Hostile labels

- **"Kharijites" as a label for Wahhabis.** (The Kharijites were an early sect that declared other Muslims unbelievers.)
  - The Hanafi jurist Ibn Abidin (died 1836) called the Wahhabis the "Kharijites of our time." He said they saw only themselves as Muslims, branded opponents polytheists and considered killing Sunnis lawful.
  - The Deobandis used his view in *al-Muhannad* to deny being Wahhabis. Ahmad Raza Khan, meanwhile, accused the Deobandis of being exactly that ([Istanbul University paper](https://avesis.istanbul.edu.tr/yayin/0d3970b7-5aaf-4254-9fae-4748034f16ff/the-effect-of-ibn-abidins-views-on-the-wahhabis-on-the-hanafi-schools-of-the-indian-subcontinent)).
  - Part of the background: Gangohi had once spoken well of Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, the founder of Wahhabism. Later Deobandi scholars put that down to hearsay ([IslamQA.org](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa/8361/shaykh-rashid-ahmad-gangohis-stance-on-the-najdis/)).
- **Pakistan, 2010.**
  - The Deobandi cleric Mufti Naeem criticised Mawlid rallies as un-Islamic.
  - Barelvi protesters in Karachi then burned his effigy and accused Deobandi clerics of Taliban links. One Barelvi leader claimed "all Deobandis are terrorists" ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/9155/the-widening-split)).

## Violence linked to the rivalry in Pakistan

Barelvi gatherings, clerics and shrines were hit by deadly attacks between 2006 and 2010.

| Date | Event | What happened | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Jul 2010 | Data Darbar bombing, Lahore | Suicide bombers killed at least 50 people at a major Sufi shrine. No group claimed it; earlier Lahore attacks were blamed on the Pakistani Taliban, who oppose shrine practices | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Data_Darbar_bombings) |
| 2010 | Attacks on Mawlid processions | Processions in Faisalabad and Dera Ismail Khan were attacked; the attacks were blamed on the banned sectarian group Sipah-e-Sahaba | [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/9155/the-widening-split) |
| 12 Jun 2009 | Killing of Sarfraz Naeemi | The Barelvi cleric was killed in a suicide attack after denouncing the Taliban | [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/9155/the-widening-split) |
| 11 Apr 2006 | Nishtar Park bombing, Karachi | A suicide bombing at a Barelvi Mawlid gathering killed at least 49 people, including the leaders of the Barelvi organisation Sunni Tehreek. Three alleged members of the militant group Lashkar-e-Jhangvi were indicted | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishtar_Park_bombing) |

Both sides have also seized each other's mosques. Deobandis have forcibly taken over Barelvi mosques and madrasas ([Middle East Institute](https://mei.edu/publication/sectarian-violence-and-intolerance-pakistan/)), and Sunni Tehreek has seized rival mosques ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/9155/the-widening-split)).

## Efforts against takfir

Two large cross-school declarations push back against takfir and sectarian violence; they are worth including for balance.

- **[Amman Message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amman_Message) (2005):** about 200 scholars from over 50 countries recognised eight legal schools. They forbade declaring followers of those schools apostates.
- **[Paigham-e-Pakistan](https://arabnews.com/node/1227141) (2018):** 1,829 Pakistani scholars from nearly all mainstream sects declared terrorism and suicide attacks un-Islamic. They said differences between sects should stay within scholarly debate ([Dawn](https://dawn.com/news/1383415/ulema-put-their-weight-behind-antiterrorism-edicts)).

## Tips for your report

- **Separate three things:** formal takfir by named scholars, hostile labelling, and violence by militant groups. None of these speaks for every member of a movement.
- **"Wahhabi" is usually an insult.** Most Salafis reject the term, and Barelvis have also applied it to Deobandis and Ahl-e-Hadith.
- **Treat partisan sites as partisan.** IslamQA, Deoband.org and similar sites are useful as evidence of each side's own position, but present them that way.
- **Use academic books for the history.** The standard works are:
  - Usha Sanyal's book on Ahmad Raza Khan (1996)
  - Barbara Metcalf's *Islamic Revival in British India: Deoband, 1860–1900* (1982)
  - Cole Bunzel's *Wahhābism* (2023)
