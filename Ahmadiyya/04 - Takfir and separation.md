# 4. Takfir and Separation

*Part 4 of 6 in a series on the main points of dispute between Ahmadi Muslims and mainstream Muslims. It describes the debate; it does not decide it.*

---

## At a glance

| Question | Critics | Ahmadiyya Muslim Jama'at | Lahore Ahmadiyya |
|---|---|---|---|
| Did early Ahmadi leaders call other Muslims *kāfir*? | Yes, clearly and repeatedly | Yes, but in the sense of "rejecting a man sent by God", not "outside the ummah of Muhammad"; and only after the ulama had first declared Ahmadis kāfir | The main community's leaders did; this departed from the founder's own stated principle |
| Why don't Ahmadis pray behind other imams? | It shows they are a separate religion | Imams who call Ahmadis kāfir, or follow those who do, cannot lead them; and the Imam of the age has come | The founder's reason was the opponents' takfir; it is not a ruling that other Muslims are non-Muslims |
| Are other Muslims Muslims? | (not applicable) | Members of the ummah of Muhammad in the general sense; Ahmadis say they have never tried to strip anyone of the name "Muslim" | Yes: everyone who recites the *kalima* |

---

## 1. Key terms

- **Takfīr**: declaring a person or group to be *kāfir* (unbeliever).
- **Kufr**: literally "covering over"; also "ingratitude" or "denial". The Quran uses it in several senses: rejecting faith, ingratitude (14:7), or not judging by revelation (5:44, about which Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to have said it is "a *kufr* less than *kufr*", *kufr dūna kufr*).
- **Millat / ummah**: the religious community.
- **Mukaffir / mukadhdhib / mutaraddid**: one who calls (Mirza Ghulam Ahmad) a kāfir / one who calls him a liar / one who is undecided.
- **Iqtidā'**: following an imam in congregational prayer.
- **Ahl al-qibla**: "the people of the qibla", all who pray towards the Kaʿba. Classical scholars used this idea to warn against easy takfir.

## 3. What Mirza Ghulam Ahmad himself wrote: two strands

**Strand 1, inclusive.** In a footnote to *Tiryaq-ul-Qulub* (October 1902) he wrote that calling someone kāfir for rejecting one's claim is the right only of prophets who bring a law and new commandments. No one becomes kāfir by rejecting the others, the *muḥaddaths*, however high their rank. He also often stressed that the takfir started with his opponents, not with him.

**Strand 2, exclusive.** In *Haqiqat-ul-Wahi* (1907) he described two kinds of *kufr*: rejecting Islam and the Prophet altogether; and, once the truth has been made clear, rejecting the Promised Messiah whom God and His Messenger foretold. Answering a questioner who quoted the earlier footnote, he argued that whoever rejects him in effect calls him a forger, and that by the hadith "if a man calls his brother kāfir, it returns to one of them" (al-Bukhārī 6103) the charge falls back on those who made it.

**How each branch reconciles the two strands:**

- **Main Jama'at:** the later writing is decisive (as with prophethood, Part 1). Both kinds of *kufr* are lesser than rejecting Muhammad. Those who do not accept Mirza Ghulam Ahmad remain within the ummah of Muhammad and under the rulings of Islam, but are at fault before God for rejecting the one He sent.
- **Lahore branch:** the 1902 footnote states his real principle. The 1907 passages concern only those who themselves called him kāfir or a liar ("the charge returns to the one who made it").

---

## 4. The second Caliph's statements, and how they are explained

The passage critics quote most often comes from *A'ina-e-Sadaqat*. In the community's own 1924 English translation, it says that Muslims who have not formally taken the bai'at, wherever they are, are "Kafirs and outside the pale of Islam", even if they have not heard the Promised Messiah's name. An earlier booklet by his brother, *Kalimat-ul-Fasl* (1915), used the phrase "*pakka kāfir*".

**The main Jama'at's explanation:**

- The word is used as the Quran uses it of those who reject a particular messenger while believing in God, not in the sense of "non-Muslim" in society or law.
- "Outside the pale of Islam" referred to the full spiritual reality of Islam as Ahmadis understand it in this age, not to membership of the Muslim community.
- Before the 1953–54 Court of Inquiry, the second Caliph explained that he used "kāfir" in this lesser sense, that non-Ahmadis were not excluded from the wider Muslim community, and that the opposing ulama had called Ahmadis kāfir first.

**The critics' reply (including the Lahore branch):**

- "Even if they have not heard his name" cannot be explained as a response to opponents' takfir.
- "*Pakka kāfir*", a complete kāfir, leaves little room for a "lesser" meaning.
- The 1953 explanation came under political pressure and was not matched by any change in practice.

---

## 5. Practices of separation

- **Congregational prayer.** Ahmadis do not pray behind non-Ahmadi imams. The reasons given:
  - the opposing ulama declared Ahmadis kāfir, so their imams, and those who follow them, cannot sensibly lead Ahmadis;
  - Mirza Ghulam Ahmad wrote (as reported in the Ahmadi paper *Badr*, December 1908) that one who calls a believer kāfir becomes one himself, so how can Ahmadis pray behind him;
  - the imam should be one who accepts the Imam whom God has sent in this age (Ahmadis cite the hadith "whoever dies without an oath of allegiance dies the death of the time of ignorance", Muslim 1851).
- **Funeral prayers.** The Jama'at's stated position is that it does not offer the funeral prayer of someone who openly called Mirza Ghulam Ahmad a liar. But if a Muslim dies where no one else will pray over him, Ahmadis would offer it (behind their own imam), because a person who professed the *shahāda* should not be buried without one. Critics point to stricter earlier statements, including one by the second Caliph applying the rule even to the children of non-Ahmadis.
- **Marriage.** The community restricts marriage outside the Jama'at, especially for Ahmadi women.
- **History.** Mirza Ghulam Ahmad prayed behind other Muslims before the fatwas began (he mentions praying the Friday prayer behind a non-Ahmadi scholar at Aligarh), and the second Caliph wrote that on his pilgrimage in 1912 he prayed behind the imams in Mecca.

---

## 6. The Ahmadi case: context

1. **"They started it."** The 1891 fatwa came first. It called Mirza Ghulam Ahmad a kāfir and a *dajjāl* (antichrist) and urged Muslims to cut off ordinary relations with his followers. Ahmadi rules about prayer came afterwards.
2. **The hadith that takfir returns on the one who makes it** (al-Bukhārī 6103).
3. **The Quran's warning:** "Do not say to anyone who offers you peace, 'You are not a believer'" (4:94).
4. **The word has many senses**, as shown above.
5. **No legal exclusion.** Ahmadis note that they have never sought laws declaring other Muslims non-Muslim, or forbidding anyone to call himself a Muslim, whereas they have been declared non-Muslim by law.
6. **The Munir Report.** The 1954 inquiry asked leading ulama to define a Muslim and found that no two of them agreed. It observed that, by the definition of any one of them, the others would be outside Islam.

---

## 7. The critics' case

1. **Mainstream:** whatever the order of events, Ahmadis who believe Mirza Ghulam Ahmad was a prophet must regard those who reject him as rejecting a prophet. Separate prayer, funerals and marriage are the marks of a separate religious community, which (critics say) justifies treating Ahmadis as one.
2. **Muhammad Iqbal** argued in the mid-1930s that the claim to prophethood made the Ahmadis a separate community, and urged the British government to declare them one.
3. **The Lahore branch:** the takfir of all non-Ahmadi Muslims was introduced by the main community's leadership and contradicts the founder's 1902 principle. Much of the documentation critics use comes from Lahore Ahmadi publications.

---

## 8. The other direction: the takfir of Ahmadis

- **From 1891:** repeated fatwas declaring Mirza Ghulam Ahmad and his followers kāfir.
- **1953:** campaigns led by the Majlis-e-Ahrar and others demanded that Ahmadis be declared a non-Muslim minority and that the Ahmadi Foreign Minister, Sir Muhammad Zafarullah Khan, be removed. Riots in Punjab led to martial law in Lahore and the Munir–Kayani inquiry.
- **1974:** In April, the Muslim World League conference in Mecca declared Ahmadis outside Islam. After violence that followed a clash at Rabwah railway station in May, Pakistan's National Assembly heard representatives of both Ahmadi groups, including the head of the main community, Mirza Nasir Ahmad, and on 7 September amended the constitution to declare them non-Muslims (Part 1, section 7).
- **1984:** Ordinance XX made it a crime, punishable by up to three years in prison, for Ahmadis to call themselves Muslims or "pose as" Muslims, to call their places of worship mosques, to give the azan, to use Islamic honorific titles for their figures, or to preach their faith.
- **Consequences:** prosecutions under these laws, and violence, for example the attacks on two Ahmadi mosques in Lahore on 28 May 2010, which killed more than 80 people.
- **A much-quoted episode:** at Muhammad Ali Jinnah's funeral in September 1948, Zafarullah Khan attended but did not join the funeral prayer, which was led by Maulana Shabbir Ahmad Usmani, a scholar of the school that had declared Ahmadis kāfir. Critics cite this as proof that Ahmadis regard other Muslims as non-Muslims; Ahmadis say he could not follow an imam who regarded him as a kāfir.

---

## 9. Summary

| Issue | Critics | Ahmadi answer |
|---|---|---|
| Early statements calling non-Ahmadis kāfir | Clear and unconditional | A lesser *kufr*: rejecting God's appointed one, not leaving the ummah |
| Who began the takfir | Irrelevant to what was said | The ulama, in 1891 |
| Not praying behind non-Ahmadi imams | Proof of a separate religion | A response to takfir, and loyalty to the Imam of the age |
| *Tiryaq-ul-Qulub* (1902) vs *Haqiqat-ul-Wahi* (1907) | Shows inconsistency (or, for Lahore, a later distortion) | The later text is decisive (main Jama'at) / the earlier one is the principle (Lahore) |
| Legal status | Ahmadis are non-Muslims (Pakistan, 1974) | Ahmadis never sought to exclude others by law; the Munir Report shows there is no agreed definition of a Muslim |
