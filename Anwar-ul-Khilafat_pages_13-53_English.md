Even now, as I have just said, I had intended to speak on other subjects; but because of the present dissension I consider it necessary to say something about two disputed questions as well. And I begin first with these points of dissension.

The first question is this: was **Aḥmad** the name of the Promised Messiah (peace be on him), or of the Holy Prophet (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him)? And does the verse of Sūrah al-Ṣaff, in which glad tidings are given of a Messenger whose name will be Aḥmad, relate to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) or to the Promised Messiah (as)?

### The Promised Messiah Is the One to Whom the Prophecy of *Ismuhū Aḥmad* Applies

My belief is that this verse relates to the Promised Messiah (as), and that he is Aḥmad. Against this it is said that Aḥmad is the name of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), and that to call anyone other than him "Aḥmad" is an affront to him. But the more deeply I reflect, the more my certainty increases, and I believe that the word *Aḥmad* which occurs in the Holy Qur'an relates to the Promised Messiah (as) alone.

By the grace of God I hold arguments in proof of this, and I am ready to present them before all the scholars and learned men of the world. I am even ready to stake a prize: if anyone can disprove my arguments and establish from the Holy Qur'an and from authentic aḥādīth that Aḥmad was the Holy Prophet's *name* and not a *descriptive attribute* —

## [Page 14]

— and that the signs of Aḥmad mentioned in the Holy Qur'an apply to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), and that the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) applied this prophecy to himself — then I am ready to pay such a penalty as may be agreed upon by both parties.

It should be kept in mind that to call the Promised Messiah (as) "Aḥmad" is no affront to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), nor does it mean that the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) was not Aḥmad. He *was* Aḥmad, and necessarily so — but Aḥmad was his *attribute*, not his *name*. Anyone who says that Aḥmad was not his attribute speaks falsely, for this is established from authentic aḥādīth. Indeed, had he not been Aḥmad, how could the Promised Messiah (as) have been Aḥmad at all — since whatever the latter attained, he attained through discipleship to him.

Yet in spite of this, it is not correct to say that the name of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) was Aḥmad; and the clamour raised by the Non-Bai'atists over this statement — that it constitutes an affront to the Messenger (ﷺ) — is entirely wrong and pointless. There is no affront to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) in it whatever. Is it an affront to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) to call Moses (as) "Moses," or an affront to call Abraham (as) "Abraham"? If it is not, then how should it become an affront to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) to call the Promised Messiah (as) "Aḥmad"?

That a particular person is the fulfilment of a prophecy is established by arguments; and when the arguments establish that the one to whom this prophecy applies is the Promised Messiah (as), on what ground is there any affront in it to the Messenger (ﷺ)?

To say that the Holy Prophet's (ﷺ) name was not Aḥmad is no affront to him, because a mere name, without the qualities the name denotes, is of no benefit. Unless the attributes corresponding to a name are found in a person, the name is nothing worthy of honour. Look: some people are named ʿAbd-ur-Raḥmān and ʿAbd-ur-Raḥīm, yet they do the work of a "servant of Satan." Likewise some bear a good and noble name, yet in reality are wicked and ill-mannered. So a name given by one's parents —

## [Page 15]

— does not of itself become something honourable. If we were to say of the Messenger (ﷺ) that the *attribute* of Aḥmad is not found in the Messenger of Allah, that would be an affront to him. But to say that his *name* was not Aḥmad can never be called an affront; it is simply a statement of fact.

So, since it is not the name but the deeds that are the means of excellence, how can there be any affront to him in not accepting Aḥmad as his name? If the Messenger's (ﷺ) name had not even been Muhammad but something else, would that have been an affront to him? Would his blessings have been any the less? Whatever his name had been, that same name would have been blessed, and the world would have been devoted to that name just as it is now devoted to the name Muhammad (ﷺ) — because people are not devoted to his *name*; in truth they are devoted to his *work*.

Therefore, if it is said that the Holy Prophet's (ﷺ) name was not Aḥmad, though the attributes of Aḥmad *are* found in him, then foolish indeed is the one who says that saying so is an affront to him.

Concerning the news given in the Holy Qur'an about Aḥmad, I have already recited the verses in which Aḥmad is mentioned. Now, by the grace of God Almighty, I shall show that in these verses the true referent of Aḥmad is the Promised Messiah (as) alone, and that the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) is a referent only by virtue of the attribute of *aḥmadiyyat*; otherwise, the man named Aḥmad about whom the news is given is the Promised Messiah (as) himself.

God Almighty says [Sūrah al-Ṣaff 61:2–6]:

> سَبَّحَ لِلّٰهِ مَا فِي السَّمٰوٰتِ وَمَا فِي الْاَرْضِ ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيْزُ الْحَكِيْمُ ۝ يٰۤاَيُّهَا الَّذِيْنَ اٰمَنُوْا لِمَ تَقُوْلُوْنَ مَا لَا تَفْعَلُوْنَ ۝ كَبُرَ مَقْتًا عِنْدَ اللّٰهِ اَنْ تَقُوْلُوْا مَا لَا تَفْعَلُوْنَ ۝ اِنَّ اللّٰهَ يُحِبُّ الَّذِيْنَ يُقَاتِلُوْنَ فِيْ سَبِيْلِهٖ صَفًّا كَاَنَّهُمْ بُنْيَانٌ مَّرْصُوْصٌ ۝ وَاِذْ قَالَ مُوْسٰى لِقَوْمِهٖ يٰقَوْمِ لِمَ تُؤْذُوْنَنِيْ وَقَدْ تَّعْلَمُوْنَ اَنِّيْ رَسُوْلُ اللّٰهِ اِلَيْكُمْ ۖ فَلَمَّا زَاغُوْۤا اَزَاغَ اللّٰهُ قُلُوْبَهُمْ ۚ وَاللّٰهُ لَا يَهْدِى الْقَوْمَ الْفٰسِقِيْنَ ۝

**[The Promised Messiah's rendering, as given in the address:]** Everything that is in the heavens and in the earth glorifies Allah, and He is the Mighty, the Wise.

## [Page 16]

O believers! Why do you say that which you do not do yourselves? This is a thing greatly disliked by Allah, that you should tell others to do what you do not do yourselves. Allah loves those people who fight in His way, drawn up in ranks, as though they were a solid, cemented structure. And [remember] when Moses said to his people, "O my people, why do you persecute me, when you know for certain that I have come to you as a Messenger of God?" So when they turned crooked, God made their hearts crooked; and Allah guides not the rebellious people.

After this incident of Moses (as), God Almighty relates an incident of Jesus (as): that Jesus son of Mary said to the Children of Israel, "O Children of Israel, I have come to you as a Messenger of Allah, fulfilling that which was said before me in the Torah, and I give you the glad tidings of a Messenger who will come after me, whose name will be Aḥmad."

Now the question arises here: who is that Messenger who came after Jesus (as) and whose name is Aḥmad? This is my own claim — and I have not made this claim without ground. Rather, it is written in just this way in the books of the Promised Messiah (as), and Hazrat Khalifatul Masih I also said the same: that Mirza Sahib is Aḥmad. Accordingly it is printed in the notes of his *dars* [Qur'anic lessons], and it is my faith that the one to whom this verse applies is the Promised Messiah (as) alone.

Yes — at first, when I heard this matter from Hazrat Khalifa I, I did not accept it at the outset, and there was much discussion about it. But when I reflected upon it, God Almighty opened my breast concerning it and granted me decisive proofs and shining arguments, and I accepted this view.

In these verses God Almighty has first mentioned Moses (as): that when he —

## [Page 17]

— came among his people, and his people caused him distress, he said to them, "I have come to you as a Messenger from God; accept me." But when they did not accept and adopted crookedness, God too made their hearts crooked.

After mentioning this, God Almighty has left out mention of all the prophets who came after Moses (as) and has mentioned only Jesus (as). The purpose of this is nothing other than this: that just as the Messiah (as) came thirteen hundred years after Moses (as), so likewise, thirteen hundred years after the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), the Promised Messiah — who is the like of Moses — would come. And the phrase *ismuhū Aḥmad* makes this clear, because the name of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) was not Aḥmad but Muhammad.

Thus, setting aside the verse under discussion, [it will be seen that] the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) has not been addressed as "Aḥmad" [in the Qur'an]; rather, only a prophecy about the Messiah (as) has been stated — which is itself the point under discussion. Anywhere in the Holy Qur'an, the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) has not been mentioned by the name Aḥmad.

Had the Holy Prophet's (ﷺ) name been Aḥmad, and had — as people relate — his mother been told this name by means of a revelation, then in the Holy Qur'an, which is itself divine [revelation], the name Aḥmad should have come first; and if Muhammad came [there], then Aḥmad should certainly have come in some places. It would be a strange revealed name indeed that the Holy Qur'an never once calls the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) by it.

The second proof that Aḥmad was not his name is this: it is not established from any ḥadīth that his name was Aḥmad. In the *Kalimah Shahādah*, upon which the whole of Islam rests, "Muhammad-ur-Rasūlullāh" is said — never "Aḥmad-ur-Rasūlullāh." Whereas, had his name been Aḥmad, there would have been at least some narration of the *Kalimah Shahādah* to the effect *ashhadu anna Aḥmad-ur-Rasūlullāh*. In the five daily calls to prayer too, his messengership is proclaimed aloud with the words *Muhammad-ur-Rasūlullāh*; never is "Aḥmad-ur-Rasūlullāh" said. In the *takbīr* [tashahhud] too it is the name Muhammad (ﷺ) of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) that occurs; and in the *durūd* too the Holy Prophet is remembered by the name Muhammad, and it is by that same name that [blessings are invoked] upon the Messenger —

## [Page 18]

— that God Almighty's mercies are invoked. Copies of the letters of the Messenger (ﷺ) exist; in all of them, in place of his signature, he affixed the seal bearing the name Muhammad (ﷺ). In not a single letter did he write "Aḥmad" as his name.

Then, the conversations of the noble Companions (ra) are recorded in the aḥādīth, yet it is not established even once that any Companion addressed the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) as "Aḥmad," nor does this name occur in their conversations among themselves. Nor is it established from history that his name was given as Aḥmad; rather, history too establishes that his name was given as Muhammad (ﷺ). All his opponents — among whom were his own relatives and his uncle — called him by the name Muhammad (ﷺ), or out of mischief called him *Mudhammam* ["the reviled one"], which is also on the metre of Muhammad.

In short, however much one may reflect and ponder, his name is found to be Muhammad and not Aḥmad — from the Holy Qur'an, from the aḥādīth, from the *Kalimah*, from the *adhān*, from the *takbīr*, from the *durūd*, from his letters, from his treaties, from history, and from the sayings of the Companions (ra). With this weight of evidence, how can one say that his name was Aḥmad?

Had Aḥmad also been his name, then in the places mentioned above his name Aḥmad would have appeared alongside the name Muhammad; at the very least he would have been addressed by the name Aḥmad in some one place, or it would have been permissible to recite in the *Kalimah Shahādah* "Aḥmad-ur-Rasūlullāh" in place of *ashhadu anna Muhammad-ar-Rasūlullāh*. But it is not so: this is established neither from the Messenger (ﷺ) nor from the Companions (ra). In the face of these facts, everyone can understand that his name was not Aḥmad.

Therefore, the Messenger named Aḥmad of whom news is given in this verse cannot be the Holy Prophet (ﷺ). Yes — if all the signs belonging to this Messenger named Aḥmad had been fulfilled in his time, then indeed we could say that "Aḥmad" in this verse means a Messenger [so called] by virtue of the attribute of *aḥmadiyyat*; for once all the signs were fulfilled in him, what reason would there be to apply it to anyone else? But that is not the case either, as I shall prove further on.

## [Page 19]

A second possibility would have been that the prophecy *ismuhū Aḥmad* contained some word on account of which we could not apply it to anyone else — for example, if it had been written that he would be *Khātam-un-Nabiyyīn*. Since only the Messenger (ﷺ) is *Khātam-un-Nabiyyīn*, and only one person can be *Khātam-un-Nabiyyīn*, we could then have said that although some of the signs were not fulfilled in their apparent wording in his time, yet since so explicit a mark is present as cannot be found in anyone but him, those other matters must bear some other interpretation, and in any case the prophecy applies to him alone. But this is not the case either. There is no word in this prophecy from which it may be established that it concerns *Khātam-un-Nabiyyīn*, nor any other word on account of which we should be obliged to apply this prophecy necessarily to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ).

Thirdly: despite his name not being Aḥmad, there could have been this reason for applying the prophecy to him — that he had himself declared that the Aḥmad mentioned in this verse was he. But this is not established from the aḥādīth. In no ḥadīth whatsoever — neither sound nor false, neither fabricated nor strong nor weak, neither *marfūʿ* nor *mursal* — is there any mention that the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) applied this verse to himself or declared his own person to be its fulfilment. So when this too is not the case, what reason is there for us to apply this prophecy to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) against the tenor of the verse?

A fourth compulsion could also have existed which would have obliged us to apply this verse to the Messenger (ﷺ) — namely, if the Gospel had given news of only one prophet, Aḥmad. In that case there would indeed have been a difficulty: if we applied this prophecy to some other person, the Messenger (ﷺ) would no longer be the one promised by the Messiah (as) — whereas it is established from the Holy Qur'an that he is the promised one of Jesus of Nazareth (as). But in the Gospel we find news of the coming of both: one, that Prophet who is the promised one of all the prophets, and whose coming is described as being, so to speak, the coming of God Almighty; and secondly, the second —

## [Page 20]

— advent of the Messiah (as). And it has been stated that first "that Prophet" would come, and then the Messiah would come a second time; and in neither of these two prophecies does the name Aḥmad occur at all.

So, if the verse containing *ismuhū Aḥmad* is applied — in accordance with the tenor of the verse — to one of the servants of the Messenger (ﷺ) instead of to the Messenger (ﷺ) himself, no other verse of the Holy Qur'an is thereby falsified, and the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) still remains the promised one of the Messiah (as). What reason is there, then, to twist and wrench the sense of this verse and force it upon him merely in order to establish that no other messenger can come after him?

Has the fear of God Almighty so departed from hearts that His word is tampered with in this way, and its meaning is plainly corrupted by putting a wrong construction upon it? Until the truth had come, the people of that time were under a certain compulsion; but now that it has been established by events that "Aḥmad" means a servant of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), to persist nonetheless in obstinacy is not the way of a believer.

### In the Gospel His Name Occurs as Muhammad (ﷺ)

Here is another strange thing: on the one hand it is insisted that the Messenger's (ﷺ) name was Aḥmad, and on the other it is argued that in the Gospel the name of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) occurs as Muhammad (ﷺ). Now, if his name in the Gospel is Muhammad, then to apply the prophecy of *ismuhū Aḥmad* to him amounts to falsifying him — for the Gospel gives news of him explicitly by the name Muhammad, whereas this prophecy gives news of a Messenger named Aḥmad. Does it not then follow plainly that the one prophecy is one thing and this another?

And does not the one who applies this prophecy to him thereby level a charge of misstatement against the Holy Qur'an — namely, that the Gospel had recorded the name Muhammad, but the Holy Qur'an states the name Aḥmad? Let such a person reflect a little on what a dangerous position this act of his places him in, and how, in order to satisfy his own inclination, he ends by falsifying both the Holy Qur'an and the Messenger (ﷺ) as well.

The Gospel in which —

## [Page 21]

— the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) is mentioned by the name Muhammad is the Gospel of Barnabas. And the late Nawab Siddiq Hasan Khan of Bhopal writes, in volume 9, page 335 of his commentary *Fatḥ-ul-Bayān*, beneath the prophecy *ismuhū Aḥmad*, that one clause of the news given in the Gospel of Barnabas is: *lākinna hādhihi'l-ihānata wa'l-istihzā'a tabqayāni ilā an yajī'a Muhammad-un Rasūlullāh* — that is, the Messiah (as) said that this dishonour and mockery of me will continue until Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, comes.

This reference dates from before our present dissensions, and it came from the pen of Nawab Siddiq Hasan Khan Sahib. It is therefore a highly reliable reference, as compared with those references which are fabricated with an eye to answering us. And this reference establishes that the name of the Messenger (ﷺ) occurs in the Gospel as Muhammad (ﷺ).

So when the name of the Messenger (ﷺ), if any, that occurs in the Gospel is the name Muhammad, what reason is there to apply this verse to him against the intent of the verse? And is not an affront thereby offered to the Messenger (ﷺ)? Indeed, is a charge not thereby laid against God Almighty — first, that the news in the Gospel had been given under one name, but the Holy Qur'an changed that very name; and second, that He gave signs which do not apply to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ)?

### The Paraclete (Fāraqlīṭ)

Our opponents adopt yet another line against us: they try to establish the prophecy of *ismuhū Aḥmad* from the news given in the Gospel concerning the Paraclete. They say that the name Aḥmad is established from "Paraclete"; and that since you do not apply the prophecy of *ismuhū Aḥmad* to the Messenger (ﷺ), the prophecy of the Paraclete will not apply to him either, but will apply to the Promised Messiah too — and if that be so, what prophecy concerning the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) will be left in the Gospel at all?

The answer to this is that the prophecy of the Paraclete does indeed concern the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), and in our view he alone is the one to whom that prophecy applies. But we do not accept —

## [Page 22]

— that if "Paraclete" is not interpreted to mean Aḥmad, then this prophecy fails to apply to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ). On the contrary, in our view this prophecy applies to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) in any case. Indeed, those who interpret "Paraclete" as Aḥmad and thereby make the Messenger (ﷺ) the fulfilment of this prophecy actually weaken their own position — because "Aḥmad" is offered as the rendering of the word *periklutos*, whereas in the extant Greek manuscripts the word is *paraklētos*. So the very word from which the meaning "Aḥmad" is derived does not exist in the present Gospels at all; and as to earlier times, it is a matter of dispute whether it was so or not. To rest an argument upon such a word, unsupported by other evidence, is an extremely weak proceeding.

Nor is it sufficient merely to say that, since the Gospel has been tampered with, the word in it must have been the one that certain people have supposed — for on that principle anyone who wished could take a verse of the Gospel and say, "It is not thus, it is so," offering as his proof that since the Gospels have been corrupted, one must accept whatever he says as correct. That corruption has occurred is one thing; that corruption has occurred in a particular place is quite another. Until it is proved *where* the corruption occurred, one cannot invent something out of one's own head and insert it into the Gospel, saying, "Originally it was this."

And we have no need to do any such thing, because *Fāraqlīṭ* is a Hebrew word, a compound of *fāriq* and *līṭ*: *fāriq* meaning "one who puts to flight," and *līṭ* meaning "Satan" or "falsehood." According to these meanings, it is the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) alone who is the fulfilment of this prophecy — for he alone is the one who put Satan to flight with spiritual weapons and rooted out falsehood, and who proclaimed aloud in the world, on behalf of God Almighty:

> وَقُلْ جَآءَ الْحَقُّ وَزَهَقَ الْبَاطِلُ ۚ اِنَّ الْبَاطِلَ كَانَ زَهُوْقًا
> *(Banī Isrā'īl 17:82)* — "And say, 'Truth has come and falsehood has vanished; falsehood is indeed bound to vanish.'"

What need have we, then, to rest the basis of our argument needlessly upon a Greek rendering which is itself under dispute? The original —

## [Page 23]

— word is *Fāraqlīṭ*, and in the light of it the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) is the fulfilment of this prophecy whether his name was Aḥmad or not. Indeed, even the word *paraklētos* found in the present Greek version — which various researchers have rendered as "comforter," "teacher," "master," or "holy spirit" — if that too be accepted, does us no harm. For after the Messiah (as), what human being came into the world as a comforter? Who claimed *yuʿallimuhum-ul-kitāba wal-ḥikmata* ["he teaches them the Book and Wisdom"]? Whom did God Almighty command to take *bai'at* from mankind — a practice not found even among the prophets before him? And since *bai'at* carries the sense of selling [oneself], he thereby became "master" as well; indeed, beyond that, even his servants became masters.

Again, who is the person who was pure through and through, from head to foot, and whom Allah has declared to be the *uswah ḥasanah* — the excellent exemplar — for the whole world? Clearly there was but one such person, and he alone laid claim to being such: namely our Holy Prophet (ﷺ) — may my father and mother be his ransom. He was that person in whom all these things were found, and who was raised for all mankind until the Day of Resurrection, so as to fulfil that condition of the Paraclete, that it would abide with people always. And which prophet is there, other than our Holy Prophet (ﷺ), belief in whom and obedience to whose commands will remain obligatory until the Day of Resurrection?

In short, the prophecy of the Paraclete has no connection with *ismuhū Aḥmad*; that prophecy relates in any case to the Messenger (ﷺ), whether its meaning corresponds to "Aḥmad" or is other than that. And even if its meaning be supposed to correspond to "Aḥmad" — was the Messenger (ﷺ) not Aḥmad in his attributes? Does anyone deny it? Indeed, the very fact that the name "Paraclete" occurs in the Gospel indicates that an *attribute* is meant here, because names are not translated, whereas attributes are translated.

So, if it be granted that in this prophecy there is an allusion to his attribute of *aḥmadiyyat*, how does it then follow —

## [Page 24]

— that the prophecy of the Paraclete contains an allusion to the prophecy of *ismuhū Aḥmad*? No connection between the two is established by any argument such as would compel us to understand both prophecies as relating to one and the same person.

Perhaps some people will bring against me the ḥadīth of Bukhārī:

> عَنْ جُبَيْرِ بْنِ مُطْعِمٍ قَالَ سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَقُوْلُ اِنَّ لِيْ اَسْمَآءً اَنَا مُحَمَّدٌ وَاَنَا اَحْمَدُ وَاَنَا الْمَاحِيْ الَّذِيْ يَمْحُو اللهُ بِيَ الْكُفْرَ وَاَنَا الْحَاشِرُ الَّذِيْ يُحْشَرُ النَّاسُ عَلٰى قَدَمِيْ وَاَنَا الْعَاقِبُ وَالْعَاقِبُ الَّذِيْ لَيْسَ بَعْدَهٗ نَبِيٌّ
> *(Mishkāt, Kitāb-ul-Ādāb, Bāb Asmā'-in-Nabī wa Ṣifātihi)*

The Holy Prophet (ﷺ) says: I have several names. My name is Muhammad; my name is Aḥmad; my name is Māḥī, in that Allah will efface disbelief through me; my name is Ḥāshir, in that people will be raised at my feet; and my name is ʿĀqib — and ʿĀqib means the one after whom there will be no prophet.

The Non-Bai'atists say that this ḥadīth proves that the Holy Prophet's (ﷺ) name was Aḥmad. But this is just like an Ārya saying that since the word *makr* is used in the Qur'an with reference to God, it follows that the God of the Muslims is a deceiver. Because the Āryas do not know that the word *makr*, when used in Urdu, is taken in a bad sense, whereas in Arabic it does not carry a bad sense, they think it objectionable — whereas in Arabic *makr* means "to devise a plan"; and since the Holy Qur'an is in the Arabic language, *makr* must be given the meaning current in Arabic, not the Urdu meaning.

The same is the case here. These people have been misled by the fact that the word *asmā'* occurs in this ḥadīth. Since in Urdu *ism* means only "name," they have supposed that all these are names of the Messenger (ﷺ) — whereas in Arabic *ism* is used to mean "attribute" as well as "name." They ought to have considered that when *ism* has two meanings —

## [Page 25]

— which of the two is to be applied here. In the Holy Qur'an, *ism* has been used in the sense of "attribute," as God Almighty says: *lahu'l-asmā'-ul-ḥusnā* (al-Ḥashr 59:25) — "To Him belong all the most beautiful names." Yet it is an established fact that God Almighty has only one personal name, namely *Allāh*; all the rest are attributive names, not personal ones. So it is established from the Holy Qur'an that *ism* also carries the sense of "attribute." Indeed, the expression *ṣifāt-e-ilāhiyyah* ["divine attributes"] does not occur in the Holy Qur'an at all; all the attributes are called *asmā'*.

And since *ism* is also used in the sense of "attribute," no difficulty remains for us in interpreting the ḥadīth. In it, the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) has enumerated his attributes — saying, in effect: these are my attributes. I am Muhammad, that is, God has praised me. I am Aḥmad, in that no other person has expressed the praise of God Almighty more than I. I am Māḥī, in that through me God Almighty is to efface disbelief. I am Ḥāshir, in that through me a gathering [*ḥashr*] will be brought about. I am ʿĀqib, in that after me there is no prophet bringing a Law.

And if, on the basis of this ḥadīth, "Aḥmad" is taken as the name of the Messenger (ﷺ), then it must also be accepted that his name was Māḥī, and Ḥāshir, and ʿĀqib as well — whereas all Muslims have, by continuous consensus for thirteen hundred years, accepted that Māḥī, ʿĀqib and Ḥāshir were his *attributes* and not his names. So when one and the same word has been used for all five, there is no reason to take it in one place to mean "name" and, with respect to another of the words, to take that same word to mean "attribute."

In short, if *asmā'* in this place is taken to mean names, then all five will have to be declared names — which is self-evidently wrong. And if they are taken as attributes, then all that this ḥadīth establishes is that Aḥmad too was an attribute of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) — and no one denies this; indeed, one who denies it cannot be a believer at all.

It is possible that someone may argue from this ḥadīth that the Messenger (ﷺ) gave an explanation of Muhammad and Aḥmad —

## [Page 26]

— no; rather, he gave an explanation of the other three names, from which it appears that the first two are his names and the other three are his attributes — since it was these that he explained. But this argument too is not sound. In the first place, the very principle is wrong that whatever is not explained must necessarily be a name; rather, an explanation is given only of that concerning which it is thought that people will not grasp the meaning.

Secondly, another narration refutes this argument as well. Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (ra) relates:

> سَمّٰى لَنَا رَسُوْلُ اللهِ صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ نَفْسَهٗ اَسْمَآءً مِنْهَا مَا حَفِظْنَا فَقَالَ اَنَا مُحَمَّدٌ وَّاَحْمَدُ وَالْمُقَفِّيْ وَالْحَاشِرُ وَنَبِيُّ الرَّحْمَةِ … وَنَبِيُّ التَّوْبَةِ وَنَبِيُّ الْمَلْحَمَةِ
> *(Musnad Aḥmad bin Ḥanbal, vol. 4, p. 395)*

That is: the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) named to us several of his names, some of which we forgot and some of which we remembered. He said: I am Muhammad, and Aḥmad, and al-Muqaffī, and al-Ḥāshir, and the Prophet of Mercy … and the Prophet of Repentance, and the Prophet of Battle.

In this ḥadīth he gave no explanation of *al-Muqaffī*, *Nabī-ur-Raḥmah*, *Nabī-ut-Tawbah* and *Nabī-ul-Malḥamah* — yet all of these are attributes. To this day no one has ever accepted them as names, nor can they be names; for his names were given by his elders, and the Arabs did not even believe in prophethood — how could they have named him "Prophet of Mercy"? In short, this ḥadīth itself proclaims aloud that what are stated in it are not the names of the Messenger (ﷺ) but his attributes.

Perhaps at this point someone may raise the question that, from the above account, it appears that the Messenger's (ﷺ) name was not Muhammad either — since Muhammad too occurs in this ḥadīth alongside the other attributes. The answer is that there is no doubt that in this ḥadīth "Muhammad" too has been mentioned as an attribute and not as a name. Yes — from the Holy Qur'an and from other aḥādīth it is known that his name was Muhammad. In this —

## [Page 27]

— ḥadīth, all that is stated are attributes. The proof of this is that in this ḥadīth the Messenger (ﷺ) spoke by way of *taḥdīth-e-niʿmat* [grateful acknowledgement of God's favour], saying, "These are my names." Now it is obvious that merely having a name is no praise. Would a man such as the Messenger (ﷺ) take pride in a mere name? God forbid! The fact is simply that here he set forth his attributes, and declared the favour of God Almighty — that He made me Muhammad, made me Aḥmad, and endowed me with other excellent qualities.

"Muhammad" too is used here as an attribute and not as a name, and by it he indicated: my name alone is not Muhammad; rather, by the grace of Allah, in respect of my deeds and my morals too I am Muhammad — one whom God has praised and whose purity the angels have proclaimed. I am he who praises God Almighty more than all others. I am he who effaces disbelief and error from the world. I am he at whose feet people will be raised. And I am he who is the last of the prophets to bring a Law.

Had this ḥadīth contained no more than "I am Muhammad and I am Aḥmad," someone might have said that these are his names and not attributes. But when, along with these, *Māḥī*, *Ḥāshir* and *ʿĀqib* also appear, it becomes clear that all of these are his attributes and not names. Hence the Non-Bai'atists' argument — that the Holy Prophet's (ﷺ) name Aḥmad is stated in this ḥadīth — is likewise proved wrong.

Otherwise, if he took pride merely in the name Muhammad — God forbid — there are a great many other people in the world bearing that name. Can they all take pride in their names, and would such pride be justified? If not, then why is this ḥadīth given a meaning that dishonours the Messenger (ﷺ) and lays upon him — God forbid — the charge that he used to boast of his names? Even an ordinary man would not do such a thing, let alone that a prophet of God, and the chief of all the prophets, should speak so. Let our opponents reflect at least this much —

## [Page 28]

— that in their opposition to us they have begun to attack even the Messenger (ﷺ). The Promised Messiah (as) too says [in a Persian verse]: *manam Muhammad-o-Aḥmad ki mujtabā bāshad* — "I am Muhammad and Aḥmad, who is the chosen one." Does this mean that all these were his names too? The name Aḥmad is at least disputed, but Muhammad was certainly never his name. Does this not then mean: in me is the attribute of *muḥammadiyyat* — and it is precisely this that can be a matter of pride? How could the mere name Muhammad have been a cause of pride for him? And the Promised Messiah's (as) name was not Muhammad at all, so that any such confusion could arise here.

Our opponents also bring forward the narration that the revered mother of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) was told that his name was Aḥmad. But this ḥadīth is false, because its narrator is a man who fabricated many thousands of false aḥādīth, and who himself confessed, "I have fabricated false aḥādīth." Moreover, the authentic narrations state that his mother was told his name was Muhammad. Thus it is written on page 62 of Ibn Hishām that his mother says: I was told in a dream that when this child is born, *sammihi Muhammadan* — "name him Muhammad." *(Ibn Hishām, vol. 1, p. 62, publisher Dār Rayḥānī, Beirut.)* See likewise *Mawāhib-ul-Ladunniyyah*.

How, then, can we place reliance on the ḥadīth of such a liar? This ḥadīth was fabricated by some one of those very people who, by their own reasoning and without authority, first applied *ismuhū Aḥmad* to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) contrary to the Holy Qur'an and the sayings of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), and who then found themselves in difficulty as to what its authority was. So they fabricated a narration. Otherwise, what reason is there that so great a matter finds no mention in the authentic aḥādīth? Why are the narrators of this ḥadīth Wāqidī and others of that same stamp, who in the view of the traditionists are liars or *munkar-ul-aḥādīth*?

In short, it is in no way established that the Holy Prophet's (ﷺ) name was Aḥmad. Now, therefore, only two possibilities remain: either it must be accepted that this prophecy relates to some other person named Aḥmad,

## [Page 29]

— or it must be granted that *ismuhū Aḥmad* likewise does not mean that his name will be Aḥmad, but that his *attribute* will be Aḥmad; and since the Messenger (ﷺ) possessed the attribute of Aḥmad, the prophecy should be applied to him in that sense.

But this device too proves unavailing, because the signs mentioned in this connection for the one bearing the name or attribute of Aḥmad are not found in the Messenger (ﷺ), as will shortly be shown. So now only one possibility remains: that this prophet bearing the name Aḥmad, or the attribute of Aḥmad — whichever it be — will be from among the servants of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), coming after him. And our claim is that the Promised Messiah (as) is that very Messenger of whom news is given in this verse.

Some people advance the following argument for Aḥmad being the personal name of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ): that in this verse Allah says the Messiah (as) had declared that this Messenger *ya'tī min baʿdī* — "will come after me." So this prophecy must refer to some such person as came first after him; and after the Messiah (as) it was the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) who came, not the Promised Messiah (as), who came after the Holy Prophet (ﷺ). Hence how can anyone other than the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) be Aḥmad?

The answer to this is that objectors of this kind raise such objections because of their unfamiliarity with the Arabic language; for they do not know that *baʿd* means "after" and not "immediately after." A thing that comes after another — whether it comes with ten things intervening, or immediately after — will still be called "after."

In the Arabic language there are only three words that express time: first, *qabl*, meaning "before"; second, *fī zamanihi*, meaning "in his time"; third, *baʿd*, meaning "after." And these same three expressions exist in every language. So one should consider which of these three words can be used with respect to the Promised Messiah (as). Can the word *qabl* be used of him — did he come before the Messiah? If not, then can the word "along with" be used — was he in his time? If that too is not so, then what word remains that can be used of him? Is it not simply the word *baʿd*? Therefore —

## [Page 30]

— if the Messiah (as) used the word *baʿd*, what harm follows from that? What other word was there that he could have used? Had the Promised Messiah (as) come before Jesus of Nazareth (as), or in his time, then indeed the word *baʿd* could have been urged as an argument against him. But when in fact he comes *after*, what objection can there be?

The Messiah (as) said that this Messenger would be after him. The only conclusion that can be drawn from this is that he would not come during the Messiah's lifetime but after his death. The conclusion can by no means be drawn that he would come *immediately* after. And besides, in what sense was the Messenger (ﷺ) "immediately after"? He came six hundred years later.

And if it be said that the condition is that, in respect of *messengership*, he came immediately after, and that this is found only in the Messenger (ﷺ), then I say that we have an equal right to say that the phrase "immediately after" applies not in respect of messengership but in respect of being Aḥmad — that is, the Messenger of whom I give news will be the first of all in respect of the name Aḥmad. So whichever earliest Messenger is proved to bear the name Aḥmad, this prophecy will have to be accepted as relating to him.

In short, if conditions are to be added, the other party has an equal right to add conditions; and if the matter is decided according to the lexicon of the Arabs, then no opponent has any ground whatever for rejecting our position. The Messiah (as) used the word *baʿd*, and the Promised Messiah (as) is indeed *after* him — neither before him nor in his time.

### The First Argument

The first argument for his being the fulfilment of this prophecy is that his name was Aḥmad; and the proofs that his name was Aḥmad are as detailed below:

## [Page 31]

### First Proof Concerning the Promised Messiah Being Aḥmad

First, in this way: that his parents gave him the name Aḥmad. The proof of this is that his father founded a village named after him and called it **Aḥmadābād**. Had his name been given as Ghulām Aḥmad, that village ought to have been called Ghulām Aḥmadābād. In the same way, a village was also founded after the name of his brother, and it is called **Qādirābād** — although he was called Ghulām Qādir. From this it appears that his name too was Qādir, and that "Ghulām" was merely prefixed to the names of both brothers as a matter of Indian custom and usage.

In India this custom has come down, that people give compound names — although such compound names carry no meaning or sense. For instance, some are named Muhammad Aḥmad, Muhammad ʿAlī, and so on, although these names have no [combined] meaning: Muhammad is one name, and Aḥmad or ʿAlī is a separate name. Joining the two produces no new advantage; the name merely becomes longer, and it is for that very purpose that these words are added. Otherwise, of the two names, only one is in reality the actual name.

In the family of Hazrat Sahib (as) too, the word "Ghulām" used to be prefixed to all names. His father's name was Ghulām Murtaḍā; his uncles' names were Ghulām Ḥaidar and Ghulām Muḥyī-ud-Dīn. In the same way "Ghulām" was added to his name — otherwise his name was simply Aḥmad, as is evidenced by his own father's testimony: that the village he founded after his name he called Aḥmadābād, and not Ghulām Aḥmadābād.

And if you insist on treating "Ghulām Aḥmad" as a compound name, it belongs to no language at all. In Arabic the name would have to be *Ghulāmu Aḥmada* — but that was not his name; and if you take it as a Persian construction, it should be *"Ghulām-e-Aḥmad"* — but his name is not that either, because in his name —

## [Page 32]

— the *mīm* carries a *jazm* [sukūn], not a *zer* [kasrah]. And if you take it as an Urdu or Punjabi construction, it should have been *"Aḥmad kā ghulām"* or *"Aḥmad dā ghulām"* — but it is not in that form either. What language, then, does this name belong to, which is attributed to Hazrat Sahib (as)?

So the truth of the matter is simply this: that his name was Aḥmad, and "Ghulām" was prefixed at the beginning merely as a family mark.

The second proof that his name was Aḥmad is that he attached "Aḥmad" to the names of all his sons. Otherwise, since attaching "Aḥmad" yielded no advantage in point of meaning, what was the benefit of adding "Aḥmad"? Clearly, Aḥmad was Hazrat Sahib's (as) own name, and it was added to the name of each of his sons as a family mark.

But he did not do so. It is therefore established that he declared his own name to be Aḥmad alone.

### Tenth Proof

The tenth proof of the Promised Messiah (as) being Aḥmad is that in the Gospel the word "Aḥmad" nowhere

## [Page 37]

occurs. So one possible explanation is that this word was effaced from the Gospel during the age of textual corruption. But there is another possibility as well, namely that the word "Aḥmad" is the Arabic rendering of some prophecy about the Messiah — and this is a matter that becomes plainly known to us from the Holy Qur'an, that the news given in different languages is stated by the Holy Qur'an in the dress of the Arabic language.

Keeping this principle in mind, when we examine the Gospel we find in it news of two Messengers: one, the news of "that Prophet," and the other, the news of the second advent of the Messiah. Now, when we consider the Arabic language, the rendering of "that Prophet" is not "Aḥmad," nor has any idiom any bearing on it. But regarding the second coming we do find an Arabic idiom, namely *al-ʿawdu aḥmad*, which means "the return is better." The reason for this is that when a man turns his attention a second time to doing something, he does it better than the first time. Thus in the Holy Qur'an too Allah has pointed to this theme:

> مَا نَنْسَخْ مِنْ اٰيَةٍ اَوْ نُنْسِهَا نَاْتِ بِخَيْرٍ مِّنْهَاۤ اَوْ مِثْلِهَا
> *(al-Baqarah 2:107)* — "Whatever Sign We abrogate or cause to be forgotten, We bring one better than it or the like thereof."

This verse tells us that when We efface one teaching and bring another, there is certainly a wisdom in it; and for that reason it is necessary that We bring some other teaching better than it — or at the least, one that is its like.

So from this verse too it appears that in doing a thing a second time, something of greater excellence is in view. And it is with just this in mind that this idiom of the Arabic language came into being: *al-ʿawdu aḥmad* — "the return is the more praiseworthy." So when the return is called *aḥmad*, then in this saying of the Messiah (as) — that "I shall come into the world a second time" — this can also be expressed in Arabic by way of metaphor: that he gave news of a Messenger whose attribute is this —

## [Page 38]

— that he will have come into the world a second time. And this meaning also accords with those prophecies of the aḥādīth in which news was given of the Messiah's second coming.

The wisdom in employing this metaphor was, first, that the prophecy contained in the aḥādīth was resolved in this way — that it is a metaphor, and that the literal return of the Messiah is not intended. And secondly, that in this one word it was also indicated that this second advent of the Messiah would be better and finer than his first advent. In this way the objection of those people was removed who ask how Mirza Sahib can be superior to the Messiah (as). God Almighty Himself answered those people: when We sent a person a second time under the same name, We also made him *Aḥmad* — that is, We also granted him excellence over the earlier Messiah.

In sum, these are the ten proofs which establish that the Promised Messiah (as) was Aḥmad, and that it was concerning him that news was given in this verse.

At this point let me also remove one further objection: perhaps someone may say that Hazrat Sahib (as) has a couplet:

> *Leave off mentioning the son of Mary — better than he is the servant of Aḥmad.*

From this couplet it appears that Hazrat Sahib's name was Ghulām Aḥmad. The answer is that in this couplet Hazrat Sahib (as) has stated his *attribute*: that I, being a servant of Aḥmad, am greater than the Messiah. From this the greatness of the Messenger (ﷺ) becomes apparent; and in this place he has not stated his name. And if a name is meant here, then what would be the meaning of this couplet:

> *Though the miracle is nameless and traceless — come and behold it among the servants of Muhammad.*

That is: although a miracle is nowhere to be seen in this age, yet come and see a miracle from the servants of Muhammad (ﷺ). Does this couplet mean, "See a miracle from those whose name is Ghulām Muhammad"? This couplet —

## [Page 39]

— does not carry that meaning; and by *ghulām-e-Muhammad* here a name is not meant but an attribute — one who is a servant of Muhammad (ﷺ). In the same way, in the earlier couplet too, "Ghulām Aḥmad" does not mean his name but his attribute.

Then it should also be borne in mind: when did we ever say that the Promised Messiah's (as) full name was not Ghulām Aḥmad? We ourselves accept that his full name was indeed Ghulām Aḥmad. But of that whole name, the essential part of the name was "Aḥmad," and "Ghulām" had merely been added as a family mark. It is for this very reason that in some places he wrote his name as Ghulām Aḥmad and in others as Aḥmad. And the real name is that smallest portion of the name which a person uses separately.

### The Second Argument

The second argument for his being the fulfilment of this prophecy is that God Almighty says:

> فَلَمَّا جَآءَهُمْ بِالْبَيِّنٰتِ قَالُوْا هٰذَا سِحْرٌ مُّبِيْنٌ
> *(al-Ṣaff 61:7)* — "But when he came to them with clear proofs, they said, 'This is manifest sorcery.'"

From this verse it appears that when that Messenger comes, people, on hearing the arguments and proofs he presents, will say, "This is manifest sorcery" — that is, plain deceit or magic. And we see that this is precisely the treatment the Promised Messiah (as) received. When he placed powerful arguments and decisive proofs before his opponents, many cried out that his words were most captivating but false. And many also said that there is such magic in his writing that it draws the reader towards itself, and that therefore one should not read it.

And although Khwaja Sahib, while delivering a lecture in Sialkot, said that since no one called Mirza Sahib a magician he is not the fulfilment of this prophecy — yet there are hundreds of men who have said that Mirza Sahib knows magic; and even now there are many who say that Mirza Sahib knew magic.

## [Page 40]

At this point someone may perhaps offer the argument that here Allah says *falammā jā'ahum bil-bayyināti qālū hādhā siḥrun mubīn* — "When that Messenger came to them with proofs, the people said, 'This is plain magic.'" So this must be some Messenger who had already come before the revelation of this verse, and that is the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) himself.

But such an objection will be raised only by a person unacquainted with the style of the Holy Qur'an; for in scores of places in the Holy Qur'an a matter pertaining to the future has been expressed in the idiom of the past — so much so that in some places the sayings of the dwellers of Hell and of Paradise have been rendered in past-tense forms. So when it is established by other arguments that this Messenger was to come at some future time, the mere fact that the passage is expressed in past-tense forms is no proof whatever that the Messenger must have come before the revelation of this verse.

### The Third Argument — On the Identification of Aḥmad

After stating in this verse that when that Messenger comes, people will call him a magician, or a liar, or a fraud and a deceiver, Allah says:

> وَمَنْ اَظْلَمُ مِمَّنِ افْتَرٰى عَلَى اللهِ الْكَذِبَ وَهُوَ يُدْعٰۤى اِلَى الْاِسْلَامِ ۚ وَاللهُ لَا يَهْدِى الْقَوْمَ الظّٰلِمِيْنَ
> *(al-Ṣaff 61:8)* — "And who is more unjust than one who forges a lie against Allah, while he is called to Islam? And Allah guides not the unjust people."

That is: who can be more unjust than one who forges a lie against Allah, while he is being called towards Islam? And Allah does not guide the wrongdoers.

In this verse an indication is given that a person who forges a lie against God Almighty deserves the severest punishment of all. Then, if this person is a liar — as you allege — he ought to perish and not to succeed. And Allah never guides wrongdoers; so when a man has, by forging a lie against God, become the worst of wrongdoers, how can He guide him? Hence this person's

## [Page 41]

— attaining progress is a sign that this person is from God Almighty and is not false, as you people allege.

In this verse God Almighty has so identified this Messenger Aḥmad that a fair-minded man can have no doubt whatever in accepting that this Messenger Aḥmad is one who is to come after the Messenger (ﷺ); that he is not himself the Messenger (ﷺ); and that no Messenger of this name has passed before him. For in this verse Allah has laid down such a condition as is not fulfilled in the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), but can be fulfilled in some prophet coming after him. The condition is this: Allah says, "Who can be more unjust than one who forges a lie against Allah, *while he is being called towards Islam*?" And this condition — that he is being called towards Islam — is a condition not found in the Messenger (ﷺ).

For the word *islām* carries two meanings, as is known from the Holy Qur'an. One: that every true religion, so long as it remains in its original state, has been given the name Islam by way of attribute. Thus all the earlier prophets who came before the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) have been called Muslims. Two: Islam is the name given to that religion which was revealed to the Holy Prophet (ﷺ).

So the phrase "while he is being called towards Islam" can bear only these two meanings: either that at that time he was being called towards the true and real religion, or that he was being called towards the religion named Islam. But we see that neither of these two applies to the Messenger (ﷺ). Because in his time there was no true religion at all except the religion he himself was following; and in the view of the disbelievers the name of the true religion was not Islam, such that it could be said to them, by way of establishing an argument against them, that "he is being called towards Islam." There remains the possibility that some religion bore the name Islam — but the fact is that this word is not found in any religion apart from the religion which the Messenger (ﷺ) brought, and the religion the Messenger (ﷺ) brought is the very religion which was named Islam.

## [Page 42]

So this condition — that if he is false, yet people are calling him towards Islam — is not found in the Messenger (ﷺ), because people were not calling him towards Islam; rather, they were calling him towards the religion of Lāt and Manāt. No one called him towards the Christian religion, nor towards the Jewish religion, nor towards the Zoroastrian religion; and there was no one who was calling him towards Islam. On the contrary, he was calling people towards the religion named Islam. So he was *dāʿin ila'l-islām* ["one who calls to Islam"], not *yudʿā ila'l-islām* ["one who is called to Islam"].

Only such a person can be called towards Islam, and towards the religion of Islam, as comes at a time when there already exists in the world a religion named Islam. And what doubt can there be that such a person can only be after the Messenger (ﷺ), since it was he himself who brought to the world the religion bearing the name Islam?

In short, the condition *yudʿā ila'l-islām* makes it plain that this person will come after the Messenger (ﷺ), and that at that time the Muslims will say to him, "Sir, why do you turn disbeliever? Abandon your claim, and do not turn your face away from Islam." In reply to this, Allah says: if he is in fact false and you are truthful, then he is a disbeliever; and you are Muslims and you are calling him towards Islam, yet he goes towards disbelief and forges a lie against God — then who can be more unjust than he? He ought to perish, because God does not guide wrongdoers either. And this is the greatest of wrongs. So since he does not perish but rather finds guidance in every field, how can he be false? And how is it possible that you should be superior in Islam and yet be disgraced?

In short, in this verse a powerful argument has been established against the enemies of the Messenger Aḥmad; and we see that the Promised Messiah (as) used to lay great stress upon the verse *wa man aẓlamu mimman-iftarā ʿala'llāh*.

Some people say at this point that *yudʿā ila'l-islām* relates not to the Messenger but to his enemies — that God Almighty is saying: who can be more unjust than one who forges a lie against Allah, while he is being called towards Islam? So in this

## [Page 43]

place the enemies of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) are indicated; and what doubt is there that they were being called towards Islam?

This idea does no doubt appear pleasing at first, but on the slightest reflection upon the Holy Qur'an its error becomes apparent — in this way: that this place speaks of a person who attributes something to God Almighty. For the very word *iftirā'* means fabricating a statement. There is this difference between *kadhib* [falsehood] and *iftirā'*: a thing will be called *kadhib* whenever it is wrong, whether the person invented it himself or heard it from someone else; *iftirā'*, however, applies to what one fabricates oneself. For example, if a person, having heard from someone that Zaid has gone to Lahore, repeats it, and Zaid has not gone, then he is a liar but not a *muftarī*. But if he invented the statement himself out of his own mind, then he is both a liar and a *muftarī*.

So since the noble verse mentions *iftarā ʿala'llāh*, it is known that it speaks of a person who says something concerning God Almighty — that is, a claimant. And nowhere in the Holy Qur'an has the word *muftarin ʿala'llāh* been used with respect to a denier; this word has been used only with respect to a claimant. Thus, wherever this word has been used even with respect to the disbelievers, their claim has first been stated. In short, the words *iftarā ʿala'llāh* make it evident that this is some claimant.

Now, when we look at these verses, we find in them no such statement of a claim by the disbelievers, in which they attribute something to God Almighty; there is merely a statement of their denial. And a denier is not called *muftarin ʿala'llāh*. Hence the disbelievers cannot be intended in this verse. Rather, it is the claimant to messengership himself who is spoken of in this verse — that if he is uttering a falsehood about God while in this condition, and he is also being called towards Islam, then why does he not perish?

Finally, to complete the argument, let me also grant this: that the statement of the disbelievers which is quoted — namely that they said, "This is plain magic" — refers to their claim. Yet no wise person would call this a claim; rather —

## [Page 44]

— it is a denial. Even so, this verse cannot be made to apply to the disbelievers, because the words *iftarā ʿala'llāh* occur in this verse, from which it is known that the person's claim relates to God Almighty, and that whatever he says he attributes to God Almighty. But to say that so-and-so, who says such a thing, is a deceiver — if that be accepted as a claim at all, it might be called *iftirā'*, but it cannot be called *iftarā ʿala'llāh*. Because this amounts to: if it is false, then it is a lie against that person who is truthful — he calls him false, and this is not an *iftirā'* against God.

But in the aforementioned verse *iftarā ʿala'llāh* is mentioned, which establishes that this verse speaks of that same Messenger of whose coming information was given earlier; and it is stated that if this person makes a false claim of messengership and forges a lie against God, then what is the reason that, despite his being of Islam, and despite his committing such mischief, God Almighty does not destroy him?

In short, in this verse it is plainly stated that this Messenger Aḥmad will come after the Messenger (ﷺ), and that the Muslims of that time will say to him, "Come towards Islam." And God Almighty will say to them, through the tongue of His Messenger: if Islam is with you, and you are calling him towards Islam, and even then he does not desist from forging a lie against God, then why is he not destroyed? And since he is not destroyed, it is known that Islam is on his side and not on yours.

Finally, I consider it appropriate to indicate this also: that the objection — that in the Holy Qur'an *wa man aẓlamu mimman-iftarā ʿala'llāhi kadhiban* has occurred in several places where there is no prophecy — is not sound. Because nowhere there is the condition *wa huwa yudʿā ila'l-islām* mentioned; that condition is stated only in this place, from which it is plain that, since here a general rule was not being stated but a prophecy was being made, therefore by adding this phrase the Messenger was to a certain extent identified as well — namely, that he would come after the appearance of Islam.

## [Page 45]

### The Fourth Argument

God Almighty says:

> يُرِيْدُوْنَ لِيُطْفِئُوْا نُوْرَ اللهِ بِاَفْوَاهِهِمْ
> — "They desire to extinguish the light of Allah with their mouths, but Allah will complete His light, however much the disbelievers may dislike it."

This verse too is a very strong argument for the Promised Messiah (as) being Aḥmad, and from it is proved that the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) is not the primary fulfilment of this prophecy — because this verse states that in the time of that Messenger people will wish to efface his Movement by their mouths.

The circumstances of the age of the Messenger (ﷺ) tell us that the attempt to efface his Movement was made not by mouths but by the sword, and such atrocities were committed that God preserve us. Very little effort indeed was made to confront Islam with arguments. So in this verse there is necessarily an indication of some other age — one in which there would be peace and security, and in which, instead of the sword, greater use would be made of tongues; and people would wish to efface the work of that Messenger by the blowing of their mouths, and would desire to obstruct his work by fabricating tales and to put a stop to his progress.

And that age is this very age, in which God Almighty has established such a just government that under its shade the lion and the goat drink water at one and the same ghāṭ; and if anyone sets about doing wrong, it seizes his hand. Accordingly, at this time our opponents have nothing in their hands but *fatwās* and abuse. By their *fatwās* they want to efface our work; but they have no such means in their hands whereby they may forcibly turn anyone away from his religion or kill him.

So this very age — in which the sword has been taken from people's hands and only the battle of mouths remains — can be the age mentioned in this verse. As for the age of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), that was an age in which Muslims were slaughtered with swords like sheep and goats; and women —

## [Page 46]

— were martyred by being struck with spears in their private parts. So that age, in which it was really the sword that was at work, and in which the opponents of Islam did not even know how to make use of arguments and proofs, cannot be the age concerning which God Almighty says that at that time people will wish to efface Islam by the blowing of their mouths.

Rather, this very age is that age — for even at this time the opponents of the Aḥmadiyya Movement do not refrain from causing it whatever distress they can. But their strength lies mostly in abuse and *fatwās*; they do not possess the power of raising a hand against them such as used to exist in earlier times.

### The Fifth Argument

> وَاللهُ مُتِمُّ نُوْرِهٖ وَلَوْ كَرِهَ الْكٰفِرُوْنَ
> — "And Allah will complete His light, however much the disbelievers may dislike it."

This verse too is a sign of the Messenger Aḥmad, and from it we learn that this verse relates to the Promised Messiah (as) — because it states that the time of Aḥmad is the time of the perfection of the light.

Now, although we know from the Holy Qur'an that a perfect Law was completed at the hand of the Messenger (ﷺ), yet the perfection of the *light* does not appear to have occurred in his time. Rather, from the aḥādīth it is known that this was to be in the time of the Promised Messiah, and that in the time of the Messenger (ﷺ) its foundation had been laid. Thus it comes in one ḥadīth that how can a people be led astray at the beginning of whom am I and at the end of whom is the Messiah. *(Kanz-ul-ʿUmmāl fī Sunan-il-Aqwāl wal-Afʿāl, Kitāb-ul-Qiyāmah min qism-il-aqwāl, nuzūl ʿĪsā ʿalā nabiyyinā ʿalaihi'ṣ-ṣalātu was-salām, vol. 7, p. 202, printed 1314 AH.)*

From this it is known that complete deliverance from the attacks of the enemies of Islam would be granted to the Muslims only at that time when the second wall — that of the Promised Messiah — would be drawn up. So the perfection of the light was destined to occur in the time of the Promised Messiah alone, and in this place too the time of the perfection of the light has been stated. Therefore in this verse it is the Promised Messiah alone who is mentioned.

And the point is also this: that those arguments in support of Islam which were placed in the Holy Qur'an and the aḥādīth —

## [Page 47]

— were like a hidden treasure, and although they existed, people were heedless of them. Now it is the Promised Messiah (as) who has come and opened them, and has granted the Muslims such a light that now the enemy cannot attack them in the dark.

### The Sixth Argument

> هُوَ الَّذِيْۤ اَرْسَلَ رَسُوْلَهٗ بِالْهُدٰى وَدِيْنِ الْحَقِّ لِيُظْهِرَهٗ عَلَى الدِّيْنِ كُلِّهٖ
> — That is: it is He, God, who sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, that He may cause it to prevail over all religions.

From this verse too it appears that it is the Promised Messiah who is mentioned here — because most commentators are agreed that this verse is in favour of the Promised Messiah, since it was decreed that Islam should prevail over the remaining religions in his time. Accordingly, events too have borne witness to this: because before this age there did not exist such means for the propagation of religion as exist now — for instance, the railway, the telegraph, the steamship, post offices, printing presses, newspapers, the abundance of learning, the abundance of trade — by reason of which people of every country move about here and there, and a person can preach in all directions while sitting in his own house, and can go wherever he wishes and accomplish the work of propagating religion. Thus we have sent our missionaries to Mauritius and to England, and there is an intention to send them to other countries too.

So this is indeed an age in which all religions can be refuted with the utmost ease. In the time of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) such conditions did not exist. In his time there was neither such a contest of religions, nor did these religions raise their heads in his day as they have now. All of this was to happen precisely in the time of the Promised Messiah (as), and so it has happened.

Then, in this age, propagation of religion by means of writing and speech was necessary for this reason also: that the people of other religions had objected against the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) that he made use of the force of the sword —

## [Page 48]

— to spread Islam. To remove this objection, God Almighty raised up one of his servants and showed that when this man can make Islam prevail over other religions by arguments and proofs, then why would his master not have done the same? So this matter too was settled: that the sword which the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) took up, he took up only because the sword had been raised against him; otherwise he too would never have taken up the sword.

In short, this verse too makes it plain that the age of the coming of that Messenger will be an age when all religions will have appeared, and such means will have come into being through which Islam can be made to prevail over all religions. And that age is this very age; and therefore it is the Promised Messiah alone who can be Aḥmad.

From this verse another thing also becomes known — namely, that in this place the Promised Messiah is mentioned. It is this: that this verse occurs in three places in the Holy Qur'an, and in all three places it comes together with a mention of the Messiah. In two places the name of the Messiah is plainly present, and in the third place the mention of the Gospel accompanies it. So this verse occurs three times in the Holy Qur'an, and in all three places its being accompanied by a mention of the Messiah indicates that this verse has some special connection with the Messiah. And that can only be this: that the subject of this verse was to be fulfilled at the time of the Messiah's second advent. For if this verse has no special connection with the Messiah, then what is the reason that in three separate places this verse has been repeated along with the mention of the Messiah — once in Sūrah al-Tawbah, rukūʿ 5; a second time in Sūrah al-Fatḥ, rukūʿ 4; and a third time in this very Sūrah al-Ṣaff?

### The Seventh Argument

> هَلْ اَدُلُّكُمْ عَلٰى تِجَارَةٍ تُنْجِيْكُمْ مِّنْ عَذَابٍ اَلِيْمٍ
> *(al-Ṣaff 61:11)*

That coming Messenger will say to people: O people, you who are bent upon the trade of this world — shall I tell you of a trade by which you may be saved from a painful punishment? This verse tells us

## [Page 49]

that in that age trade will be very powerful; people will have forgotten religion and become engrossed in worldly trade. And this indeed is that age, in which worldly trade is so abundant as it has never been in any earlier age.

This is the very reason why the Promised Messiah (as) took the *bai'at* in these words: that I shall give precedence to religion over the world. So this verse too proves that it is the Promised Messiah (as) alone who is mentioned in these verses.

### An Incidental Point

> تُؤْمِنُوْنَ بِاللهِ وَرَسُوْلِهٖ وَتُجَاهِدُوْنَ فِيْ سَبِيْلِ اللهِ بِاَمْوَالِكُمْ وَاَنْفُسِكُمْ ۚ ذٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ اِنْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُوْنَ
> *(al-Ṣaff 61:12)*

God Almighty says: O people, believe in Allah and His Messenger, and strive in the way of Allah with your wealth and your lives. That is very good for you, if you but knew.

There are many people who, having given a subscription, suppose that they are now discharged and that no duty remains upon them. But here God Almighty says: spend your wealth *and* your lives too — that is, both give subscriptions and also preach. So the people of the Aḥmadiyya Community ought to act accordingly. In this verse God says: if you spend your wealth and also preach, it will be very good for you — that is, you will progress rapidly.

> يَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ ذُنُوْبَكُمْ وَيُدْخِلْكُمْ جَنّٰتٍ تَجْرِيْ مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْاَنْهٰرُ وَمَسٰكِنَ طَيِّبَةً فِيْ جَنّٰتِ عَدْنٍ ۚ ذٰلِكَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيْمُ ۝ وَاُخْرٰى تُحِبُّوْنَهَا ۚ نَصْرٌ مِّنَ اللهِ وَفَتْحٌ قَرِيْبٌ ۚ وَبَشِّرِ الْمُؤْمِنِيْنَ
> *(al-Ṣaff 61:13–14)*

God Almighty will forgive you your sins and your past lapses, and will admit you into gardens beneath which streams flow, and there will be, for dwelling in, great and pure places —

## [Page 50]

— in the gardens [of Eternity]. This will be a very great success for you. And one further thing will fall to your lot, which you desire: namely, the help of God will come to you, and there will be a swift victory. And this is glad tidings for the believers.

### The Eighth Argument

After this, God says:

> يٰۤاَيُّهَا الَّذِيْنَ اٰمَنُوْا كُوْنُوْۤا اَنْصَارَ اللهِ كَمَا قَالَ عِيْسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ لِلْحَوَارِيّٖنَ مَنْ اَنْصَارِيْۤ اِلَى اللهِ ۚ قَالَ الْحَوَارِيُّوْنَ نَحْنُ اَنْصَارُ اللهِ فَاٰمَنَتْ طَّآئِفَةٌ مِّنْۢ بَنِيْۤ اِسْرَآءِيْلَ وَكَفَرَتْ طَّآئِفَةٌ ۚ فَاَيَّدْنَا الَّذِيْنَ اٰمَنُوْا عَلٰى عَدُوِّهِمْ فَاَصْبَحُوْا ظٰهِرِيْنَ
> *(al-Ṣaff 61:15)*

O you people who have believed in the Messenger — become helpers of the religion of God Almighty, just as Jesus son of Mary said to the disciples, "Who are my helpers in the cause of Allah?" They said, "We are the helpers of Allah." So one party of the Children of Israel believed and another party disbelieved. Then We aided those who believed against their enemies, and they became victorious.

In this there is the argument that the coming Messenger will say to people, "Become *Anṣārullāh* [helpers of Allah]." But the call of the Messenger (ﷺ) was not, "O people, become *Anṣār*." Rather, in his time there were two groups, the *Muhājirīn* and the *Anṣār*, and the group of the *Muhājirīn* held superiority over the *Anṣār*.

Thus it is known from the aḥādīth that after the battle of Ḥunain, when a great deal of booty was received and he distributed it, by way of conciliating hearts, among the new Muslims of Mecca, some of the *Anṣār* objected — saying that up to now blood has been dripping from our swords, but the Messenger of Allah has given the property to others. And some even went so far as to say that now he will join his own people. When he heard this, he gathered the *Anṣār* in one place and said: "O *Anṣār*, news about you has reached me — have you found any evil in me? Were you

## [Page 51]

not astray, and God Almighty guided you through me? And did I come among you when you were not poor, and God Almighty made you wealthy? And were you not enemies of one another, and Allah made you friends?"

The *Anṣār* submitted: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah! It is indeed so, by the grace and favour of Allah and His Messenger." Then he said, "O *Anṣār*, why do you not answer me?" They submitted, "What answer shall we give?" He said: "Had you wished, you could have said — and your statement would not have been false — that 'You came to us at a time when people were rejecting you, and we accepted you as true. And no one was with you, and then we helped you. And you had been driven out, and we gave you a place. And you were poor, and we showed you sympathy.'

"O *Anṣār*, have you taken it ill in your hearts on account of the goods of this world, by means of which I conciliated the hearts of a new people? O *Anṣār*, are you not pleased that people should take goats and camels to their homes, while you take the Messenger of God to your homes? By that God in whose hand is my life, had there been no *hijrah*, I would have liked to be one of the *Anṣār*. And if people were to go into one valley and the *Anṣār* into another valley, I would go into the valley into which the *Anṣār* had gone. O God, have mercy on the *Anṣār*, and on their sons, and on their daughters."

At this the *Anṣār* wept so much that their beards became wet. *(Bukhārī, Kitāb-ul-Maghāzī, Bāb Ghazwat-il-Ṭā'if …)*

From this narration it is known that in the time of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) *hijrah* held a lofty rank; and in the Holy Qur'an too there is special emphasis on *hijrah*. So had the age of the Messenger (ﷺ) been intended, then before "*Anṣār*" there would have been a mention of *hijrah*, and it would have been written, "Enter among the *Muhājirīn* and the *Anṣār*." But in this place there is no mention of *hijrah* at all — from which it is known that this is such an age as one in which *hijrah* will not be obligatory. And that is this very age.

### The Ninth Argument

In the chapter following this one — which stands immediately beside it — God Almighty says:

> هُوَ الَّذِيْ بَعَثَ فِي الْاُمِّيّٖنَ رَسُوْلًا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُوْا عَلَيْهِمْ اٰيٰتِهٖ وَيُزَكِّيْهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتٰبَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ ۖ وَاِنْ كَانُوْا مِنْ قَبْلُ لَفِيْ ضَلٰلٍ مُّبِيْنٍ
> *(al-Jumuʿah 62:3)*

And after it He says:

> وَّاٰخَرِيْنَ مِنْهُمْ لَمَّا يَلْحَقُوْا بِهِمْ ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيْزُ الْحَكِيْمُ
> *(al-Jumuʿah 62:4)*

— and He will raise this Messenger among another people who have not yet joined you.

In these verses there is mention of two raisings of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ). And since, according to the aḥādīth, there is mention of a Messiah after him, concerning whom he went so far as to say that he will be buried in my grave — that is, he and I shall be one and the same being — from this it is known that by the second raising the Promised Messiah alone is meant. For Islam does not believe in transmigration, that one should imagine he will himself come again; therefore by his second raising only this can be meant: that some person will come dyed in his colour. And that can be none other than the Promised Messiah, concerning whom he said that he will be buried in my grave. *(Mishkāt, Kitāb-ul-Fitan, Bāb Nuzūl ʿĪsā ʿalaihi's-salām)*

Now, when we place the earlier chapter alongside this one, we find that in it too there is first the mention of Moses (as) and then of the Messiah (as). Then in this chapter there is mention of the two raisings of the Holy Prophet (ﷺ), of which one took place in the manner of the Messiah's advent. Putting these two things together, it becomes plain that the prophecy of Aḥmad in the first chapter is there to convey precisely this: that just as a like-of-Moses has appeared in this Ummah, so a like-of-the-Messiah will also appear under the name of Aḥmad. Accordingly, to make this clear, He mentioned in Sūrah al-Jumuʿah the two raisings of the Messenger (ﷺ), so that a wise person may understand that by Aḥmad is meant his *second* raising and not the first — because before this the account of Moses (as) has already been stated,

## [Page 53]

and the Holy Prophet (ﷺ) is the like of Moses (as).

In short, by placing Sūrah al-Jumuʿah alongside Sūrah al-Ṣaff, God has made the prophecy of *ismuhū Aḥmad* still clearer. And the matter is perfectly plain: whether anyone accepts it or does not accept it, that is his own choice.

By the grace of God Almighty, you people who are believers in the Promised Messiah (as) are Companions of Aḥmad, and are believers in the second raising of the Messenger (ﷺ). At this time there is no other community that can match you. At this time God Almighty has made the Aḥmadiyya Movement of the same colour as the Companions (ra); and this is the one community that bears every kind of pain, hardship and affliction, and yet does not desist from spreading the religion of God, nor grow weary.

There is no doubt that, in order to give you courage and to make you work with greater zeal, it is said to you that there is this deficiency in you, this defect, this weakness. But you alone in the world are such a community as has no parallel — and it is you, and you alone, who are the complete model of the Companions (ra). Only a blind man now could say that you are not dyed in the colour of the Companions (ra).

Although at this time you are poor and weak in the eyes of the world, yet in the sight of God you are very strong. In the eyes of the world you are lowly, but before God your rank is very great and you possess much honour. Therefore those days are coming when God Almighty will make you successful and fulfilled in the eyes of the world as well, and the world will see with its own eyes the prophecy concerning the Community of Aḥmad being fulfilled in you.
