# Full-body workout plan (3 days a week)

## Why full body instead of one muscle per day

| | Full body (3 days) | One muscle per day (5–6 days) |
|---|---|---|
| Each muscle trained | ~3× a week | 1× a week |
| Gym days needed | 3 | 5–6 |
| If you miss a day | Small impact | A muscle goes untrained for 2 weeks |
| Best for | Beginners to intermediates, busy people, fat loss, general health | Advanced bodybuilders with lots of time |

- Training a muscle at least twice a week builds more muscle and strength than once a week, for the same total amount of work.
- Big compound lifts burn more calories and build the muscle that raises your resting metabolism, which is what lowers metabolic age.
- 3 days a week is realistic to keep up long term, and consistency matters more than the perfect program.
- Later on (after 12+ months of consistent training), you can move to a 4-day **upper/lower split**, which still trains each muscle twice a week.

## Weekly schedule

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Full body A |
| Tue | 30–40 min brisk walk |
| Wed | Full body B |
| Thu | Walk, or 15–20 min easy cycle or swim |
| Fri | Full body A |
| Sat | Badminton, a long walk, or any sport |
| Sun | Rest |

Swap A and B the following week (B, A, B). Aim for 8,000–10,000 steps a day overall.

## The workouts

### Full body A (about 55–60 min)

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Goblet squat (or barbell squat) | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec–2 min |
| 2 | Dumbbell bench press (or push-ups) | 3 × 8–12 | 90 sec |
| 3 | Seated cable row (or dumbbell row) | 3 × 10–12 | 90 sec |
| 4 | Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec–2 min |
| 5 | Face pulls | 2 × 12–15 | 60 sec |
| 6 | Plank | 3 × 30–45 sec | 60 sec |

### Full body B (about 55–60 min)

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trap-bar deadlift (or deadlift) | 3 × 6–8 | 2 min |
| 2 | Overhead dumbbell press | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec |
| 3 | Lat pulldown (or assisted pull-ups) | 3 × 8–12 | 90 sec |
| 4 | Walking lunges (or reverse lunges) | 3 × 10 per leg | 90 sec |
| 5 | Farmer's carry | 3 × 30–40 m | 60–90 sec |
| 6 | Dead bugs (or hanging knee raises) | 3 × 10–12 | 60 sec |

### Optional extras (if you have 10 minutes spare)

Do these as a superset (one after the other, then rest) at the end of either session:

- Dumbbell biceps curls: 2 × 10–12
- Cable triceps pushdowns: 2 × 10–12
- Standing calf raises: 2 × 12–15

---

## Before every session: warm-up (5–8 min)

1. 5 min of easy cardio (bike, cross-trainer or brisk incline walk)
2. 10 bodyweight squats
3. 10 hip hinges (hands on hips, push hips back)
4. 10 arm circles each way, 10 band pull-aparts if a band is available
5. 1–2 light sets of your first exercise (about 50% of your working weight)

---

## Reading the plan

- **3 × 8–10** = 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps.
- **Choosing the weight:** the last 2 reps of each set should feel hard while your form stays clean. If you can easily do more than the top of the range, it's too light.
- **Speed:** lower the weight slowly (2–3 seconds), then lift with control. No bouncing or swinging.
- **First 2 weeks:** go light and learn the movements.

## Exercise instructions: Full body A

### 1. Goblet squat (or barbell squat)
**Works:** thighs (quads), glutes and core.

1. Hold one dumbbell upright at your chest with both hands cupping the top end.
2. Feet slightly wider than hips, toes turned out a little.
3. Brace your stomach and keep your chest up.
4. Sit down between your heels, pushing your knees out in line with your toes.
5. Go down until your thighs are about parallel to the floor, or as low as you can with a flat back.
6. Push through your whole foot to stand up.

**Avoid:** knees caving in, heels lifting, rounding your back, leaning too far forward.
**Starting weight:** 10–16 kg. Move to a barbell squat once ~25–30 kg feels comfortable.

### 2. Dumbbell bench press (or push-ups)
**Works:** chest, front shoulders and triceps.

1. Sit on a flat bench with dumbbells on your thighs, lie back and bring them to chest level.
2. Feet flat, shoulder blades squeezed back and down into the bench.
3. Press up until your arms are straight, without banging the dumbbells together.
4. Lower slowly to chest level, elbows at about 45° from your body.

**Push-ups:** body straight from head to heels, chest to a fist's height from the floor. Start on your knees or with hands on a bench if needed.
**Avoid:** elbows flared wide, big lower-back arch, bum lifting off the bench.
**Starting weight:** 10–14 kg per hand.

### 3. Seated cable row (or dumbbell row)
**Works:** upper and mid back, rear shoulders and biceps. Balances out desk posture.

**Cable row:**
1. Feet on the platform, knees slightly bent, chest up, back straight.
2. Pull the handle to your belly button, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
3. Return slowly until your arms are straight.

**Dumbbell row:**
1. One hand and the same-side knee on a bench, back flat like a table top.
2. Pull the dumbbell towards your hip, elbow close to your body.
3. Lower slowly. Do all reps on one side, then switch.

**Avoid:** jerking your body back, shrugging your shoulders, rounding your back.
**Starting weight:** 25–35 kg on the cable stack, or 12–16 kg dumbbell.

### 4. Romanian deadlift (RDL)
**Works:** hamstrings, glutes and lower back. Great for posture and back health.

1. Stand holding dumbbells (or a barbell) in front of your thighs, feet hip-width apart.
2. Keep a slight knee bend and don't change it.
3. Push your hips back, letting the weights slide down the front of your legs.
4. Keep your back flat and chest proud. Go down until you feel a strong hamstring stretch (usually mid-shin).
5. Squeeze your glutes and drive your hips forward to stand up.

**Avoid:** rounding your back (most important), bending your knees into a squat, weights drifting away from your legs.
**Starting weight:** 12–16 kg dumbbells per hand.

### 5. Face pulls
**Works:** rear shoulders and upper back. Protects your shoulders and counters hunching at a desk.

1. Set a cable pulley at upper-chest to face height with a rope attachment.
2. Hold the rope ends with palms facing in, step back until your arms are straight.
3. Pull the rope towards your face, splitting the ends apart so your hands finish beside your ears.
4. Squeeze your shoulder blades together for a second, then return slowly.

**Avoid:** using too much weight, leaning back, shrugging.
**Starting weight:** light, 10–20 kg on the stack. This is a control exercise, not a heavy one.

### 6. Plank
**Works:** your whole core, including the deep muscles that protect your back.

1. Forearms on the floor, elbows under shoulders.
2. Legs straight back, resting on your toes.
3. Body in one straight line from head to heels.
4. Squeeze your glutes, brace your stomach, breathe normally.

**Avoid:** hips sagging, hips too high, holding your breath.
**Progression:** once 45 seconds is easy, go to 60 seconds or lift one foot off the floor.

## Exercise instructions: Full body B

### 1. Trap-bar deadlift (or deadlift)
**Works:** legs, glutes, back, grip and core. The best single strength exercise.

Start with the trap bar (the hexagon-shaped bar). You stand inside it, so it's easier on your lower back and easier to learn.

1. Stand in the middle of the bar, feet hip-width apart.
2. Push your hips back and bend your knees to grab the handles. Back flat, chest up.
3. Take a big breath into your belly and brace hard.
4. Push the floor away with your legs and stand up tall. Squeeze your glutes at the top without leaning back.
5. Lower under control: hips back first, then bend your knees.
6. Reset your breath and brace before every rep.

**Avoid:** rounding your back, yanking the bar, leaning back at the top, hips shooting up first.
**Starting weight:** 40–60 kg total. Add 5 kg a week while your form stays good.

### 2. Overhead dumbbell press
**Works:** shoulders, triceps and upper chest, plus core if standing.

1. Stand (or sit on a bench with back support) with dumbbells at shoulder height.
2. Brace your stomach and squeeze your glutes so your back doesn't arch.
3. Press straight up until your arms are almost straight.
4. Lower slowly back to your shoulders.

**Avoid:** leaning back, pressing forward instead of up, bouncing with your legs.
**Starting weight:** 8–12 kg per hand.

### 3. Lat pulldown (or assisted pull-ups)
**Works:** lats (the wide back muscles that give a V-shape), biceps and grip.

**Lat pulldown:**
1. Thighs locked under the pads, grip slightly wider than shoulders.
2. Lean back very slightly and pull the bar to your upper chest, driving your elbows down to your ribs.
3. Let the bar up slowly until your arms are straight.

**Assisted pull-ups:** use the assisted machine or a resistance band. Chin over the bar, lower slowly. Reduce assistance over time.
**Avoid:** pulling behind your neck, swinging, letting the bar fly up.
**Starting weight:** 30–40 kg on the stack.

### 4. Walking lunges (or reverse lunges)
**Works:** thighs, glutes and balance. Fixes left/right imbalances.

1. Stand tall, dumbbells at your sides (or none at first).
2. Take a long step forward and lower your back knee towards the floor. Both knees at about 90°.
3. Front knee over your ankle, torso upright.
4. Push through your front heel and step into the next lunge.
5. 10 steps per leg (20 total) = 1 set.

**Avoid:** short steps, front knee caving in, leaning forward.
**Starting weight:** bodyweight in week 1, then 6–10 kg per hand. If space is tight, do reverse lunges on the spot.

### 5. Farmer's carry
**Works:** grip, core, shoulders, traps and posture. Also gets your heart rate up.

1. Pick up a heavy dumbbell or kettlebell in each hand (use your legs, flat back).
2. Stand tall with your shoulders back and down, core braced.
3. Walk with short, controlled steps for 30–40 m.
4. Put the weights down carefully, rest, repeat.

**Avoid:** leaning to one side, shrugging, rushing.
**Starting weight:** 16–20 kg per hand. Build towards half your bodyweight in total.

### 6. Dead bugs (or hanging knee raises)
**Works:** deep core muscles. Very safe for the lower back.

**Dead bug:**
1. Lie on your back, arms pointing straight up, knees bent at 90° above your hips.
2. Press your lower back into the floor and keep it there.
3. Slowly lower your right arm behind your head and straighten your left leg towards the floor.
4. Return, then switch sides. Each side = 1 rep.
5. Move slowly and breathe out as you extend.

**Hanging knee raise (harder):** hang from a bar and lift your knees to your chest by curling your hips, without swinging.
**Avoid:** lower back lifting off the floor, rushing, swinging.

## How to progress

- **Keep a log** of weight and reps for every exercise (phone notes, or a free app like Strong or Hevy).
- **Double progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets, add weight next session (1–2 kg per dumbbell, 2.5–5 kg on bars and machines) and work back up the range.
- **Lighter week every 6–8 weeks:** cut sets to 2 and drop the weight by about 20% for one week. This helps your joints recover, and you'll usually come back stronger.
- **Cardio:** walking and the Thursday session cover most of it. If you want more, add 10 minutes of intervals on the bike after a session (30 sec hard, 90 sec easy, × 5).
- **Soreness** for 1–2 days after your first few sessions is normal. Sharp joint pain is not, so stop that exercise if it happens.
- **Form check:** one session with a gym personal trainer on squats, RDLs and deadlifts is worth the money.
- **Reassess after 3–4 months.** If you're progressing, keep going. After 12+ months, consider a 4-day upper/lower split.
